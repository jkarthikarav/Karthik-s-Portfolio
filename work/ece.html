Work Experience Highlights - WORKING AT The Edward S. Rogers Sr. Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, UNIVERSITY OF TORONTO

Making ECE’s Digital Experience Easier to Navigate

Between June 2024 and March 2025, I worked with the University of Toronto’s Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE), initially managing up to 60 website production requests per month before expanding into end-to-end UX research, design, and development across its digital ecosystem. Starting in November 2024, I worked on improving how prospective students, researchers, faculty, and industry partners discovered and explored information across ECE’s web properties.

Skip Ahead to Work

Core Responsibilities

ECE’s website served prospective students, graduate applicants, faculty, researchers, and industry partners. However, important programs, research opportunities, events, and industry connections were not always easy to discover.

Improved Content Discovery

Restructured core pages to make ECE’s programs, research opportunities, industry partnerships, and events easier to find.

Clearer Content Hierarchy

Organized information around what visitors needed to discover and understand, rather than relying solely on existing page structures.

Created dedicated pathways for industry collaboration and student research opportunities that were not adequately supported by the previous content experience.

What I Did

I focused on two things: Understanding who the experience needed to serve, and Building responsive & reusable components and UI patterns across all web properties.

ECE’s website served prospective students, graduate applicants, faculty, researchers, and industry partners, but important information and opportunities were not always easy to discover.

I interviewed stakeholders, reviewed engineering department websites across North America, and spoke informally with students and faculty. This helped me prioritize two audiences: prospective undergraduate students choosing a major and graduate applicants evaluating ECE’s research and industry connections.

These insights established a North Star for the work: make ECE’s programs, research, and opportunities easier to discover while helping visitors understand what the department offers. I used this as a decision framework for prioritizing content and evaluating design choices.

I designed and implemented modular components that adapted across phone, tablet, laptop, and desktop screens. Rather than treating responsive design as a final implementation step, I considered how content hierarchy, navigation, and layouts should work across viewports.

The components could also be duplicated and adapted by non-designers through content edits, helping maintain consistency as ECE’s digital ecosystem expanded. Patterns developed through this work were subsequently reused for other ECE web properties.
