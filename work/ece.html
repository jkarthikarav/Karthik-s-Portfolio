University of Toronto's Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering

Between June 2024 – March 2025, I worked with the ECE Department, managing up to 60 production requests per month before expanding into end-to-end product design across its web properties. Starting November 2024, I embarked on a project where I researched, designed, developed, and shipped improvements across ECE's digital ecosystem.

Project period: Nov 2024 — Mar 2025

Reworking ECE's digital experience around discovery

ECE's website needed to serve prospective students, graduate applicants, faculty, researchers, and industry partners, but important information and opportunities were not always easy to discover.

The goal wasn't to reinvent the department's identity. It was to understand where the existing experience could better support discovery and engagement, then design and ship improvements within ECE's existing digital ecosystem.

Understanding who the experience needed to serve

Defining the audiences the website needed to serve

I interviewed stakeholders, reviewed competitor department sites across North America, and spoke informally with students and faculty. From this, I prioritized two audiences: the 17-year-old choosing a major and the graduate applicant evaluating the institution, its research, and industry connections.

This gave the project a consistent decision framework: does this section or copy serve prospective students?

Selected Work

THE HOME PAGE - ECE.UTORONTO.CA/

Restructuring the homepage around first-time visitors

I restructured the homepage from the ground up into independent modular sections designed around the needs of first-time visitors. Each section created a clearer path into content that could help prospective students evaluate ECE, from programs and student life to research and industry opportunities.

Making overlooked opportunities visible to prospective students

The department was active in industry, but the website did not clearly communicate those relationships. I created an Industry Opportunities page with dedicated Work with us and Sponsor us pathways and departmental metrics, making those connections visible to both partners and prospective students.

Industry Opportunities →

Research access was similarly important to graduate applicants, but the existing Research at ECE experience had no way to host student job postings. I created a Student Research Opportunities page to make those opportunities discoverable.

Student Research Opportunities →

Turning repeated patterns into a reusable design system

I built pages from modular components that could be duplicated and adapted by non-designers through copy edits rather than recreated from scratch. These patterns were designed to remain consistent and responsive as the ECE ecosystem expanded, and were later used for connected properties including TNFC and the Grid Modernization Centre.

This scalability was useful in my next big project for the ECE, where I had to integrate a standalone departmental web property (TNFC lab) inside ECE's website.

Components built for both the TNFC and the pages built as part of this project were then reused for the the integration of another departmental web property (Grid Modernization Centre) after I left.

THE EVENTS PAGE - /EVENTS

Reframing the Events page around what visitors needed to understand

The existing /events experience was simply an auto-generated calendar. I reframed the page around what visitors needed to understand before browsing individual events, introducing category cards, contextual information, and the full calendar through an embedded widget.

Integrating TNFC into ECE's digital ecosystem

TNFC had operated as an isolated web property. I redesigned its experience as an ECE content hub, applying the same focus on discovery, information architecture, reusable patterns, and responsive design.

TNFC Case Study →

Treating responsive design as part of the experience

I treated responsiveness as part of the experience rather than a final implementation step. Flexible layouts, reusable components, and adaptive content maintained consistent navigation and visual hierarchy across phone, tablet, laptop, and desktop.

This approach transforms a website into an ongoing information guide while respecting the trust users place in their university during one of the most important periods of their lives.
