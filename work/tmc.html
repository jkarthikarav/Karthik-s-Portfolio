Oct — Nov 2023 UX Research

Improving Content Discovery Through UX Research

A student team project for the Textile Museum of Canada, focused on improving website navigation and content discovery. Important content, including ticket sales, donations, archived events, and programming, was difficult to find.

Investigating the Navigation Problem

We used card sorting and tree testing to understand how users grouped content and navigated the existing website.

  1. 01Only 30% of participants located the Collection Gallery.
  2. 02Card sorting produced between 4 and 10 categories, revealing no consistent mental model for the existing content structure.
  3. 03Tree testing helped validate a revised information architecture in a controlled environment.

A research-validated IA would not automatically guarantee better navigation once implemented within a redesigned interface.

Translating Research into a Solution

We developed a revised navigation structure with clearer labels, more intuitive groupings, and direct pathways to important content.

Rather than recommending an immediate Mega Menu redesign, I proposed a two-phase implementation:

  1. 01Phase A: ValidationIntroduce the revised IA within the existing menu and evaluate its impact.
  2. 02Phase B: OptimizationIntroduce the Mega Menu after the revised IA demonstrates improvement.

I also designed the high-fidelity navigation concepts for both phases.

Presentation & Feedback

We presented our research and recommendations to the museum's CEO and one other Board member on November 30, 2023. We also provided a research summary handout so stakeholders could review the findings.

Our phased recommendation stood out among the student presentations. The CEO specifically appreciated having multiple options and a practical short-term solution.
