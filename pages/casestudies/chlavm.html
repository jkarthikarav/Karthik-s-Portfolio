Discovery Study for Website Redesign

Diagnosing systemic website usability failures in Virtual museum website (CHLAVM).

CHLAVM is a virtual-only museum, meaning their website is their entire institution. We undertook a discovery study to guide a landing page / full website redesign.

My role spans Research Design, Survey Instrumentation, Competitor & Accessibility Analysis, and Synthesis & Reporting

The core problem we set out to solve was: Is the CHLAVM website organized to support a first-time culturally curious user?

UX Research

Jan 23 – Feb 12, 2025

Client

Canadian Hispanic-Latin American Virtual Museum

Tools

Figma, Miro, FigJam

Research Methods

Survey, Interviews, Accessibility Audits

Research and Approach


—

The questions were designed so that instead of asking users to tell us their frustrations, we mapped user mental models directly to user interface designs such as placement, labeling, hierarchy, context.

Competitor Analysis across 3 categories: virtual-only museums, physical museums with virtual exhibits, and Latin American museums.

Accessibility Audit of the current website against WCAG standards to identify compliance failures.

Heuristic Analysis of the landing page's layout, content hierarchy, and navigation.

Survey (18 responses) to generate responses about navigation, content, and engagement.

One-on-one remote Zoom sessions with targeted usability tasks including exhibit discovery and locating media.

Why is a website redesign critical for CHLAVM?

For a physical museum, the website is a supporting channel. Often a way to plan a visit, browse a collection, buy a ticket. For CHLAVM, the website is the entire experience.
But, Nonprofits operate within funding cycles, board approvals, and grant processes that determine when and how recommendations can move forward.
This was also a pro bono academic UX capstone engagement, so the decision to act on the recommendations was an organizational one.

Summary of Findings


—

The Canadian Hispanic-Latin American Virtual Museum is a virtual-only cultural institution dedicated to preserving, promoting, and sharing the heritage of Hispanic and Latin-American communities in Canada through interactive exhibits using virtual reality.

1

The navigation reflects the museum's internal logic, which meant users could not predict what they would find before clicking.

2

The landing page has no hierarchy, including an overcrowded hero banner and undifferentiated video lists producing cognitive overload.

3

Critical actions, such as donation, required signups , something users found confusing.

4

Content, such as ideo exhibits, is presented without context including a lack of titles, descriptions, or framing.

5

CHLAVM has more distinctive cultural content than most museums, but they simply lack the system to deliver it.
