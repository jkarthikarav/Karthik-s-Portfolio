Musical Education Technology Design: Note4U

Designing a business plan for a AI-powered product that will aid music education

Sep - Dec 2024 • Role: UX Research, Business Process Modeling • Academic Project: JDM3619 Digital Media Distribution • Delivered a Business Plan and a Strategy Presentation

Project Context

The goal of this academic project was to design a technology product and business concept for musicians. This interdepartmental academic project was conducted in collaboration with the Faculty of Music and the Faculty of Information at the University of Toronto, and included 3 teams in total. We, as a team, chose to design a product that helps students and amateur producers translate musical ideas into usable sheet music or MIDI files.

Domain & Focus

Our team decided to focus on creating a business plan for a digital product that addresses gaps in music education. We decided to explore the challenges musicians face when converting audio ideas into sheet music or digital formats, with an aim to make music creation more accessible for students and creators.

Many musicians, students, and amateur producers struggle with translating music into sheet music or MIDI either due to limited technical knowledge, lack of accessible tools, and fragmentation in their workflows. This slows down their creative process and limits opportunities for learning as well as collaboration.

Our Vision and Ethos

Note4U is a concept app that encourages the growth of artistic merit on both individual and communal levels. Our intention with partnering with universities and schools is to encourage a student’s growth trajectory. Working with these prospective institutions and creatives at the level they’re at in order to encourage growth into sustainable careers. We do this by allowing people of all levels to use our software and present the information we need efficiently and accessibly.

We believe that a subscription-based model stymies creative drive and squeezes revenue from the user-base. For a company that emphasizes student learning, we view that as unethical.

While programs such as Ableton or Logic can do rough MIDI transcriptions, we believe that to best respect other actors within the music industry, we have to approach them where they are.

Solution Approach

Our team designed Note4U, a concept for a music education technology product that allows users to convert musical ideas into sheet music or MIDI files. The solution combines digital audio input, automated transcription, and an intuitive interface to simplify music creation for students, amateur producers, and musicians.

User Research & Insights

We conducted secondary research on existing music transcription tools, studied educational gaps in music programs, and interviewed potential users including students, amateur producers, and professional musicians. Key insights included:

Users struggle with manually transcribing musical ideas due to limited knowledge of sheet music or music theory.

Current tools are often too complex, not intuitive, or not integrated with commonly used DAWs.

Many music students lack consistent instruction in transcription, creating barriers for learning and creativity.

Users expressed a desire for tools that simplify music transcription and enhance creative workflows.

Competitors

Standalone Companies: RipX and Lalal.ai

These companies develop and distribute their own tools independently, without partnerships with DAW or VST manufacturers. While details about their legal frameworks are unclear, they likely rely on external legal consultation to protect their products and operations.

Companies with Audio-Splitting Features

Some DAW manufacturers, such as Logic Pro and FL Studio, offer built-in audio-splitting tools. However, since these are tied to the DAW purchase, users can only access such features within those platforms.

Partners

Native Instruments

A leading manufacturer of VST plug-ins and virtual instruments, Native Instruments could distribute Note4U as a VST plug-in within their Kontakt library. Musicians would benefit from seamless integration, and the product would remain a one-time purchase with no recurring fees.

Musescore

Partnering with Musescore could help us reach their large base of users who browse, share, and listen to sheet music online. Our product links could be embedded on their platform, prompting users who can’t find the sheet music they need with a call-to-action: “Can’t find it here? Try Note4U.”

UX Design Process

While UX research and UI design are usually part of product design, in our case we focused purely on understanding our potential users to shape our business proposal. We wanted to figure out who might be interested in our product, what challenges or pain points they faced, and how we could position and market our product to them effectively.

This research was not about designing the product itself, but about identifying the right audience and understanding their needs. By mapping out user behaviors, frustrations, and preferences, we were able to make informed decisions about our target market and how to communicate the value of the VST in a way that resonates with them.

Persona Creation

Alex, the Aspiring Producer

Meet Alex,
the Aspiring Producer!

"I need accurate sheet music fast so I can rehearse without delays."

Alex is an aspiring music producer and first-year music student at U of T whose main challenges are playing instruments by ear and transcribing music, and they value plug-ins and software that are easy-to-use and supports their learning and passion. Alex needs to find a platform that can help generate accurate sheet music so that they can organize, rehearse and perform a song without any delays. They also need intuitive tools or software to assist in transcribing music so that they can focus on creative music production tasks.

Current User Journey (As-Is)

Alex, our aspiring producer at U of T, created a song in their DAW for a course project and needed sheet music to rehearse and perform it live with other musicians. They first tried to transcribe the song by ear, but limited knowledge of notation made the process slow and frustrating. Looking for help, Alex reached out to peers, mentors, and online transcription services, eventually obtaining the sheet music but at a high cost and with added dependency on others. During rehearsals, errors in the transcription caused interruptions, leaving Alex stressed about the time and money lost in preparing for the performance.

Next Steps in Product Design and Build

I think if we went forward with the Note4U VST design, I’d approach it using an iterative design process that keeps users’ goals and requirements at the center while helping us create a clean, intuitive, and functional interface.

Based on what we’ve learned from research, I’d start by sketching out wireframes with a logical control layout where related features are grouped together so it feels natural to use. From there, we could build a prototype to simulate real interactions like navigating through the interface or exporting sheet music. We’d test, refine, and repeat this process until we reach a solid MVP.

Usability testing would play a big role in shaping layout and functionality decisions, such as whether we should create modular, task-based sections or how users interact with features like exporting transcribed sheet music, using MIDI controls, or processing audio. The goal would be to make sure every knob, slider, panel, and view feels purposeful and clear within the overall visual hierarchy.

Once the design feels right, I’d hand it off to the development team to build the fully functional VST. I’d take a modular design approach throughout so it’s easier to expand and refine later. The goal would be to deliver a minimum viable product that feels polished, practical, and user-centered.
