ECE Homepage Redesign

Turning an academic homepage into a student recruitment portal

    March 2025 • Role: UX/UI and Web Developer • Work for: ECE Department at the University of Toronto • Mockups + WordPress Development using Figma, WordPress

Summary: Case Study detailing the redesign and WordPress implementation of the University of Toronto ECE department homepage.

The ECE department wanted a homepage that would engage prospective students while reflecting its identity.

While working on the website's production, I executed the homepage redesign end-to-end: conducting UX research, designing wireframes, prototyping in Figma, presenting iterations to stakeholders, and implementing the final site on WordPress including animations & custom CSS and JS.

Much of the previous content was outdated, some from 2022, and navigation was cluttered, making it hard for students to find programs, events, or co-op opportunities. The department asked for a homepage that was visually compelling, modular for future updates, and aligned with university branding.

My Process

Unlike other pages I built for the ECE department, such as the Events or Industry Opportunities pages, the homepage was an existing page that required a redesign. My work involved updating and modernizing the structure, improving content visibility, and implementing interactive features, rather than building it from scratch. I approached it as a 1→n product, ensuring modularity so future updates and campaigns could be easily implemented.

1. Content Audit & Discovery Research

I reviewed other FASE faculty sites and leading engineering department portals, identifying outdated content, missing calls-to-action, and hierarchy issues.

2. Synthesizing Insights, Ideating Elements, Building Wireframes

Wireframes were created in Figma to map content hierarchy and flow, emphasizing narrative pacing and guiding them through key information.

3. Stakeholder Presentations & Refining Draft Builds

I presented designs to department leadership and iterated based on feedback from admins, students, and marketing staff.

4. Complete Build on WordPress to LIVE

I implemented the designs on WordPress, building modular sections, integrating animations, and adding custom code where needed.

Designing the Homepage Narrative

The narrative I created for the homepage guides visitors on a clear journey.

Hero Section → First Impressions

A video combined with department information communicates energy & identity to students.

The goal was to show that ECE is exciting and accessible, and to whom the department is for.

Programs & Events → Engagement

Modular cards highlight the department’s programs and upcoming events.

The flow encourages exploration and naturally guides users to action.

Research & Co-op → Opportunity

Spotlight cards, making opportunities visible to students and industry partners alike.

Modular Layout → Flexibility

A consistent modular structure ensures updates are easy and the narrative remains active as new events and opportunities are added.

The homepage tells a story to the user. It starts with identity, highlights opportunities, and ends with actionable content. Each scroll reinforces the department’s credibility and relevance for students.

To highlight the department’s achievements, I proposed and implemented a top bar with key stats:

1. Among the top ECE departments in Canada and the world
2. ECE has secured over 135 patents in the past five years
3. Over 33% of ECE students are women

This information had previously been hidden inside a smaller section inside the home page as a standard bullet point, but the bar ensures students and partners immediately see the department’s strengths. It reinforces credibility while keeping the design clean and visually engaging.

What I Learned

Switching from website production and building new pages to redesigning the homepage taught me how research, design, and technical implementation on a 1→n product come together to tell a user-focused story. Unlike pages I built entirely from scratch, the homepage involved rethinking and refining existing content. Working with legacy material reinforced the importance of shaping a cohesive, engaging narrative for users. Every page has a journey, and careful design ensures that students can explore

Key Takeaway: Everything on a page must matter

A page is not just a collection of content and a bold banner at the top with a CTA. It has to be a cohesive narrative that guides users.
