ECE Homepage Redesign

Turning an academic homepage into a student recruitment portal

    March 2025 • Role: UX/UI and Web Developer • Work for: ECE Department at the University of Toronto • Mockups + WordPress Development using Figma, WordPress

Summary: Case Study detailing the redesign and WordPress implementation of the University of Toronto ECE department homepage.

The ECE department wanted a homepage that would engage prospective students while reflecting its identity.

While working on the website's production, I executed the homepage redesign end-to-end: conducting UX research, designing wireframes, prototyping in Figma, presenting iterations to stakeholders, and implementing the final site on WordPress including animations & custom CSS and JS.

Much of the previous content was outdated, some from 2022, and navigation was cluttered, making it hard for students to find programs, events, or co-op opportunities. The department asked for a homepage that was visually compelling, modular for future updates, and aligned with university branding.

My Process

Unlike other pages I built for the ECE department, such as the Events or Industry Opportunities pages, the homepage was an existing page that required a redesign. My work involved updating and modernizing the structure, improving content visibility, and implementing interactive features, rather than building it from scratch. I approached it as a 2.0 as opposed to a 0→1 or 1→n design task.

Designing the Homepage Narrative

I finalized the homepage narrative and identified the key elements needed to tell the story effectively. I focused on creating a modular structure that would make content easy to update while keeping the homepage dynamic and engaging.

The sections I designed include:

Hero Section → First Impressions
Programs → Engagement
Events → Connection
Research & Co-op → Opportunity

The Events section was originally just the Hear from the Chair section inherited from the old home page (it connected to the About page of the Department), but the decision to add the events calendar widget was made later. It was added during the second iteration after the initial implementation, as the Events page that I was building was completed only later.

Each section was built as modular blocks in WordPress, giving me (and after I leave, the content team) the flexibility to update videos, images, cards, CTAs, and widgets independently.

Designing Sections based on Narrative

Hero Section

Prospective students and partners need to quickly understand what makes the ECE department unique and engaging. They also need easy access to the most important resources on the website.

Pictured below: What it looked Like(L) and Wireframe(R)

A dynamic video paired with department information conveys the ECE at U of T identity. The video was implemented as a Youtube Embed link for easy switching based on the latest campaign the department would wish to run.

Pictured below: Approved High Fidelity Wireframe(L) and Variation Implemented(R)

A prominent key stats bar highlights the department’s achievements upfront; visitors immediately see that ECE ranks among top departments in Canada and globally, the 135+ patents secured in the past five years, and that over 33% of students are women.

A resources bar provides direct links to the website’s four top sections: News, Faculty, Teaching Labs, and Events.

Previously, achievements and resources were buried in smaller sections. The hero section now ensures visitors instantly perceive the department’s strengths and can reach core content quickly.

Programs

Prospective and current students need an engaging way to explore undergraduate and graduate programs.

Pictured below: What it looked Like(L) and Wireframe(R)

Modular cards highlight undergraduate and graduate programs; each card includes an image and a CTA that links directly to the respective program page, encouraging exploration and guiding users to action.

Pictured below: Approved High Fidelity Wireframe(L) and Variation Implemented(R)

Previously, all stats were presented in simple paragraph and bullet format; the program cards with images and CTAs make information easier to absorb and provide a clear action to the users.

Research and Industry Opportunities

Prospective students and industry partners need a clear way to explore research opportunities and potential collaborations.

Pictured below: What it looked Like(L) and Wireframe(R)

Two modular cards highlight Research at ECE and Industry Opportunities; each card includes a short blurb and a CTA that links directly to the respective page, encouraging exploration and guiding users to action.

Pictured below: Approved High Fidelity Wireframe(L) and Variation Implemented(R)

Previously, the section included eight research group cards, all linking to the same page, which created clutter and redundancy. The new design consolidates information into two direct and simple to understand cards, providing clear actions for both prospective students and industry partners.

What I Learned

Switching from website production and building new pages to redesigning the homepage taught me how research, design, and technical implementation on a 1→n product come together to tell a user-focused story. Unlike pages I built entirely from scratch, the homepage involved rethinking and refining existing content. Working with legacy material reinforced the importance of shaping a cohesive, engaging narrative for users. Every page has a journey, and careful design ensures that students can explore.

Key Takeaway: Everything on a page must matter

A page is not just a collection of content and a bold banner at the top with a CTA. It has to be a cohesive narrative that guides users.
