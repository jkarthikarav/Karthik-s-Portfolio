Gen-AI Service Blueprint for Film Pre-Visualization

Implemented July 2023

Role: Service Design

Client: Fix It In Post Studios

FigJam, Adobe Suite

Summary: Case Study detailing the Service Design of a Gen-AI-assisted Film Pre-Production workflow for Fix It In Post Studios, presented to studio directors.

Gen-AI dropped in Adobe Beta in May 2023; by July, we were making money with it.

I worked with the studio in summer 2023 for a short term freelance service design project. The challenge was to explore Adobe Photoshop's Generative AI tool and design a workflow that could make concept videos faster and more precisely; while keeping humans at the center of the creative process.

By July 2023, we had a working AI-assisted workflow and had delivered a client project.

Disclaimer: The workflow was applied on a live client project, serving as a pilot to validate the AI-assisted process with real-time feedback from clients, design teams, editor, studio heads, and the accounts manager.

Understanding the Challenge

As part of the Studio's Pre-Production support services for Film directors (including other services I created like pitch deck websites such as this), I led the development of a scalable, AI-assisted pitch video workflow.

When it comes to Indian film concept videos, the traditional process can feel like a maze. Ideas evolve constantly, goals remain unclear, and each round of client feedback can send the team back to square one. Timelines stretch out, frustration builds, and even the most talented directors and creatives struggle to stay aligned.

Having spoken to a lot of directors and video editors, a typical project followed this process:

Our goal was to change that. We wanted a process that moved faster and kept everyone on the same page, while also experimenting with AI to replace the mundane.

Some might read through this and say, “This isn’t a fully automated AI workflow.... What use is it?” But that is exactly why it matters.

Fully automated AI workflows were impractical, as clients often provided vague instructions, requiring hours of review and fine-tuning on the designer-side. My approach focused on using AI to reduce time spent on tedious tasks, allowing the team to concentrate on creative priorities. Research from 2025 confirms that entirely AI-generated work can be more time-consuming without optimized prompts and output quality (MITRIX, 2025; METR, 2025).

Designing the Workflow

We began by mapping the ecosystem; identifying stakeholders, systems, and touchpoints to understand the complexity of the pitch video environment.

Next, we created an initial concept workflow; small and testable. Real-time collaboration with a client allowed us to observe challenges directly and adjust processes immediately.

Logical Service Flow

Over multiple iterations, we refined a service blueprint. We clarified internal roles for directors, editors, designers, and coordinators. We also established clear handoffs, export conventions, and feedback loops, validating the workflow through observational research and team feedback.

This project was focused primarily on internal service design, refining workflows, roles, and a pilot application of this workflow, serving as both a deliverable for Fix It In Post Studios and a validation of the AI-assisted process for their clients (Film and TV Directors, for the time being).

At the Studio-Client level

In the typical process mentioned earlier, the Filmmaker retains a lot of creative control, as well as the headaches. This is mostly due to a lack of trust and the various inconsistencies in the interactions which freelance editors might provide/have provided them in the past. The studio must maintain control while building client confidence to ensure satisfaction and repeat projects. The traditional process could also affect employee morale and influence perceptions of design skill (despite this being a customer choice). To deliver a trust-inducing customer experience, we had to focus on the breadth, depth, and consistency of interactiosns provided by the studio teams. This informed the logical flow outlined above.

After a few weeks of brainstorming and iterative refinement, the Studio Head, Design Team Lead, and I defined employee responsibilities and began the training process.

Editor (Collaborating with the Client): selected visual references and defined tone and pacing.

PM & Design Team Lead: assigned work roles, breaking down assets and tasks for designers. Here, Red is to be removed, Green is to be kept.

Service Blueprint

The final blueprint reflects a tested system that balances operational discipline with creative freedom. The process depicted here has been rigorously validated over a 2.5-month period through research, observational studies, and workshops I facilitated. It documents workflows, responsibilities, handoffs, and AI integration in a human-centred way. Since its adoption, it has been actively used with minor updates to accommodate AI tool improvements and project-specific needs.

Impact & Future Considerations

The outcomes were tangible:

↦ Directors could visualize concepts efficiently, even on limited budgets.
↦ Teams worked more aligned and efficiently, reducing revisions.
↦ A reusable foundation for future pre-production services.

What I Learned

I delivered an AI-assisted workflow for a service offering that let creators turn ideas into videos was established. It involved me using core tenets and processes from UX research, service design, CX design, and more to create a service offering which is human-centred in process and output from start to finish.

Key Takeaway: Applying UX and Service Design in AI-assisted Workflows

This project allowed me to apply UX and service design skills entirely within business development and internal workflow processes; a context I hadn’t considered before, as service design is often taught for real-time, physical services (like restaurants, POS kiosks, or in-person interactions).
