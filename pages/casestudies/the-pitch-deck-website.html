The Pitch That Outlasted the Film It Was For

A film director needed to raise capital in a market where newcomers were being turned away on professionalism grounds. He asked for a pitch deck; FIIP Studios called for a different medium, assessed feasibility, built the system, and made it into a pitch deck website.

The Context

Newcomers weren't losing on talent. They were losing on presentation and technical innovation.

In the Indian film industry, gatekeeping had become codified through concerns of a lack of professionalism.

Senior producers were citing it to reject newcomers not as a critique, but based on how they showed up.

Outside of streaming platforms, most directors had never been asked to prepare anything beyond a narrated/bound script. Film bibles were "Netflix" concepts & Pre-production documentation was not a norm.

Our client, a director had two completed films.

But that wasn't enough in a room of influential producers who'd seen too many underprepared pitches, especially with COVID when markets were hit critically.

He needed to walk in and immediately express a different standard; To fit the base requirements, he asked for a PowerPoint.

Discovery Session

A PPT Deck was doable; But was that all we could do?

My Team and I discovered the real requirements: He was planning on walking it through, possibly on different laptops on different operating systems each time and/or a projector if needed. He also wanted a background score to set the atmosphere.

What we knew after the Discovery Session: A PDF solved the cross-platform visual & formmatting problem of PPTs; It didn't solve for the lack of music.

What We proposed: A password-protected web application solved for both primary requirements and allowed us to control interactivity and pacing. The investor would be guided, like a software demo.

The Karthik Approach

My team and I made the call to build a website. My job was to determine whether it was executable and then make it. My team aided with content design.

Feasibility was the name of the game.

Three constraints shaped every decision that followed.

The content was under strict NDA; therefore the site needed a security layer.

The movie was a Period-based Spy Drama, like Man from U.N.C.L.E. and Argo, so the UI had to reflect that.

The solution also had to be lightweight, cross-browser, and require close to nothing from the host device.

A CMS was rejected purely because the client wasn't updating this, and the added complexity wasn't justified.

I opted for performance and convenience for the presenter. Every cut was a feasibility AND design decision.

User Flow

To maintain exclusivity (a key psychological driver for investors), the site begins with a secure login, leading into an "invisible ink" environment.

Step 1: Secure Entry Point: A password screen opened the experience. After entering, they were treated to a thematic invisible ink environment.

Step 2: Thematic Reveal: A plane-shaped button triggered the score and unveiled the full pitch; music was tied to user action, not autoplay.

Fig 1. "To-Be" workflow: User journey flow for the pitch web experience: login to reveal sequence.

Designing the Solution

I sat down with the Director, learnt what the film was about and designed a solution that fit the theme and the technical requirements.

The experience opened on a password screen.

Past the gate, an invisible ink interaction revealed the narrative. Audio triggered after the core user interaction, not before; working within browser autoplay policies without breaking immersion.

The transition states were handled in JavaScript, sequenced so that the atmospheric score landed at the moment the content did.

The visual system was built from redacted documents and retro Cold War typography. The genre had to be understood in the first three seconds, before any investor read a line of copy.

Result

Did the film get funded and made? No. But the pitch created multiple partnerships.

The director's project didn't get funded: the film's plot itself had problems that no format could solve. But the production house was impressed enough by how he showed up that they brought him on for their future project slate.

2 years later, after I had left FIIP Studios, one of those future projects came to me directly as a freelance pitch deck request. The production house had already sent multiple projects down FIIP Studios' way.

This is what the project actually achieved. It wasn't to fund one film, but to demonstrate that a newcomer director and by extension the startup agency/studio behind his materials, operated at a different standard.

Most Indian video production startups/studios were typecasted as inexpensive post-production finishing houses that did CG and rotoscoping work. This was evidence that FIIP Studios shouldn't be.

What I learned

The biggest technical problem on this project was engineering an audio-visual flow that respected browser autoplay restrictions without disrupting immersion. Solving it required understanding the policies well enough to work within them, not around them.
The broader lesson was that UX doesn't belong only to SaaS and e-commerce. HCD Principles like reducing cognitive load, controlling the user's journey, and designing for the actual environment not the assumed one, can work for any problem. I believe they can be used in any room where someone needs to leave knowing they have a solution and the implementation will help them acheieve their goals.
There is one unresolved question this project left me with: is the new generation professionalism problem in any industry actually an image problem? And how many gatekeepers would lower the bar if the work simply looked like it belonged?
