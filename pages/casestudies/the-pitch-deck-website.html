The Pitch Deck Website

The Karthik Approach

A film director needed to raise capital for his film and asked for a pitch deck; Working at FIIP Studios, My team and I called for a different medium, assessed feasibility, built the system, and made it into a pitch deck website.

The Context

—

In the Indian film industry, gatekeeping had become codified through concerns of a lack of professionalism.

Senior producers were citing it to reject newcomers not as a critique, but based on how they showed up. Outside of streaming platforms, most directors had never been asked to prepare anything beyond a narrated/bound script. Film bibles were "Netflix" concepts & pre-production documentation was not a norm.

Our client, a director, had two completed films. But that wasn't enough in a room of influential producers who'd seen too many underprepared pitches, especially with COVID when markets were hit critically.

He needed to walk in and immediately express a different standard; to fit the base requirements, he asked for a PowerPoint.

Discovery Session

—

A PPT deck was doable; but was that all we could do?

My team and I discovered the hidden requirements when he explained that the pitch might be walked through on different laptops, operating systems, and possibly even a projector depending on the meeting setup.

What we knew after the Discovery Session was that a PDF solved the cross-platform visual and formatting issues of PowerPoints, but did not solve the lack of music or atmosphere.

What we proposed instead was a password-protected web application that solved both primary requirements while letting us control pacing, interactivity, and presentation flow like a software demo.

Design Priorities and Feasibility

—

These constraints shaped every decision that followed.

1

The content was under strict NDA, therefore the site needed a security layer before anything else could be considered.

2

The movie was a period-based Spy Drama inspired by titles like Man from U.N.C.L.E. and Argo, so the UI needed to visually reflect that tone and atmosphere.

3

The technical requirement was that the solution had to remain lightweight, cross-browser, and require almost nothing from the host device.

4

A CMS was ultimately rejected because the client had no intention of updating the site, making the added complexity unnecessary.

I opted for performance and convenience for the presenter. Every cut was a feasibility AND design decision.

User Flow

—

To maintain exclusivity, a key psychological driver for investors, the site begins with a secure login, leading into an "invisible ink" environment.

1

Secure Entry Point: A password screen opened the experience. After entering, they were treated to a thematic invisible ink environment.

2

Thematic Reveal: A plane-shaped button triggered the score and unveiled the full pitch; music was tied to user action, not autoplay.

Fig 1. "To-Be" workflow: User journey flow for the pitch web experience: login to reveal sequence.

Designing the Solution

—

The experience opened on a password-protected screen, past which an invisible ink interaction revealed the full pitch. Audio triggered after this "reveal" interaction, not before; working within browser autoplay policies without breaking immersion.

The transitions were handled in JavaScript, sequenced so that the atmospheric score landed with the content. The visual system was built from redacted documents and retro typography. The live link remains under NDA. A data-scrubbed low-fidelity prototype is available below.

What Happened After

—

Did the film get funded and made? No. But the pitch created multiple partnerships.

The director's project didn't get funded: the film's plot itself had problems that no format could solve. But the production house was impressed enough by how he showed up that they brought him on for their future project slate.

Two years later, after I had left FIIP Studios, one of those future projects came to me directly as a freelance pitch deck request. The production house had already sent multiple projects down FIIP Studios' way.

This is what the project actually achieved. It wasn't to fund one film, but to demonstrate that a newcomer director — and by extension the startup agency/studio behind his materials — operated at a different standard.

Most Indian video production startups/studios were typecasted as inexpensive post-production finishing houses that did CG and rotoscoping work. This was evidence that FIIP Studios shouldn't be.

What I Learned

—

The biggest technical problem on this project was engineering an audio-visual flow that respected browser autoplay restrictions without disrupting immersion. Solving it required understanding the policies well enough to work within them, not around them.

The broader lesson was that UX doesn't belong only to SaaS and e-commerce. HCD principles like reducing cognitive load, controlling the user's journey, and designing for the actual environment not the assumed one, can work for any problem. I believe they can be used in any room where someone needs to leave knowing they have a solution and the implementation will help them achieve their goals.

There is one unresolved question this project left me with: is the new generation professionalism problem in any industry actually an image problem? And how many gatekeepers would lower the bar if the work simply looked like it belonged?
