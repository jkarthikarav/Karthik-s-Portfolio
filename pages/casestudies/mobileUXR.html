UX/UI Research: Mobile App for Ticket Deals

Translating UX Research insights into Mobile UI designs

    Oct - Dec 2023 • Role: UXR + UI Design • Mobile UI Mockups using Figma, Miro

Summary: We (a team of 6 UX/UI Designers) studied accessibility challenges for Torontonians in the arts and culture domain, focusing specifically on how people discover and use museum ticket deals.

Our Goal

Reduce fragmentation and create a mobile-first platform where deals are easy to discover and act upon.

Secondary Research

The focus of our background research was to understand how accessible cultural heritage institutions are for Torontonians. We aimed to learn about public awareness of museum admission prices and discount programs, as well as perceptions around affordability and access. This research helped frame our understanding before conducting primary research.

We reviewed online news articles, blogs, social media posts, and community forums to understand how museum discounts are communicated and perceived. Many Reddit discussions centered on users discovering free or discounted museum admissions and sharing this information with others; often expressing surprise at not knowing about these opportunities earlier. While academic sources were limited, city websites and news platforms provided valuable data on ticket pricing, attendance, and accessibility initiatives.

Our secondary research revealed that while free and discounted admission opportunities exist across Toronto, they remain underutilized due to low public awareness and scattered communication. This insight shaped our project’s direction: to design a UX-friendly solution that centralizes and simplifies access to museum discounts, improving cultural accessibility and inclusivity for Torontonians.

Primary Research

We conducted primary research with participants aged 18 and above living in Toronto who are interested in visiting museums. This included a survey with 42 participants (36 eligible responses) and 12 qualitative interviews and contextual inquiries to understand user behavior, motivations, and challenges when interacting with museum information, particularly around tickets and discounts.

Survey results indicated that most participants actively search for ticket and discount information online, with a median effort rating of 2.14 on a 1-5 scale. Many reported that easier access to discount information would increase their likelihood of visiting the museum, and most would recommend this information to friends.

The qualitative interviews revealed that participants visit the museum primarily for culture, history, and entertainment, and that half plan their visits in advance. Common challenges include difficulty finding discount information, and there is a strong desire for increased exposure to promotional content to encourage attendance.

UI Designs

I (with one other member of the UXR team) designed mobile UI screens and a prototype to make ticket deal surfing and sharing easy through:

1. Ease of Access & Speed

Insight: Users want quick, centralized access to all ticket discounts.
Design: Centralized feed with all available discounts in one screen and Save & Share CTAs for instant action.

2. Planning by Date

Insight: Users plan visits by specific dates.
Design: Calendar-based visual planner for easy date selection.

3. Trust & Clarity

Insight: Users trust interfaces that are clear and organized.
Design: Offers grouped visually with recognizable icons for quick comprehension.

4. Relevant Recommendations

Insight: Users need personalized, relevant content.
Design: AI-powered recommendations based on previous behavior.

UI Design - High Fidelity

Validation

The purpose of this was to determine the usability of the low-fidelity prototype that we did as a team (note the internal team review and panel presentation -- this was after that but before my solo revisit). The goals were to assess how effectively users could perform tasks, collect performance data, and gather feedback on issues. We used observation and interview methods in moderated online sessions via Zoom, where users shared their screens while interacting with the prototype. Participants were aged 18+ and had either visited a Toronto museum in the last 6 months or were interested in visiting one.

Results

What I Learned

The goal of this project was to create a clean, functional UI that simplifies how Torontonians discover and access museum ticket deals.

Designing around real-world fragmentation (many museums, different systems) required a UI that could bring coherence without overwhelming the user. Through visual hierarchy, smart filtering, and minimal navigation, we created a prototype that felt both local and user-friendly. This project also sharpened my feature prioritization skills using an impact-feasibility matrix, and reinforced the value of designing for trust.

Also, I envisioned it as an AI-powered interface: its recommendations and event highlights would adapt based on user preferences and behaviour data, improving relevance over time.

Key Takeaway: Make messy systems feel simple

When back-end data is scattered or inconsistent, design clarity is non-negotiable. A well-structured interface can build trust by masking complexity, guiding users straight to what they value.
