The Challenge

ECE at U of T maintained a web presence that had become outdated and unfocused, featuring legacy content, competing calls to action, and a lack of clear departmental identity.

93% of high-school students use a college’s or university’s website during their decisionmaking journey. [Source]

Given this, the homepage needed to function as a recruitment tool first. I was the Lead Designer on this project, making UX/UI decisions that led to improved information flow and accessibility across the homepage and resulted in funneling traffic into pages that provided most value.

The Karthik Approach

First, I examine. This typically involves a Content Audit and Discovery Research, including fellow faculty sites and other engineering websites to define industry-standard patterns, establish a hierarchy, and identify content gaps.

Half of internet users in this 2025 study  say that they use website design as a factor to formulate their opinion on a company's brand.

With visual design directly influencing brand perception, the solution had to look like it belonged alongside peer departments.

Simply comparing the site to its peers across the university such as: Faculty of Applied Science and Engineering , Mechanical & Industrial Engineering , and Chemical Engineering , confirmed that the branding was inconsistent and needed alignment.

Most key design decisions were guided by stakeholder queries and requests.

I was in touch with department admin and various stakeholders from co-op, undergraduate, graduate, and events teams to define the structure and fix the Content Architecture.

— Core Design Decisions & Priorities

After my research, I decided to

Faster Time-to-Value

Prioritize first-time users over information density to significantly accelerate initial user orientation.

Clear Primary User

Position prospective students as the primary segment over faculty and partners for better acquisition.

Lower Cognitive Load

Focus on an extended vertical scroll over immediate discoverability in favor of progressive disclosure.

The Karthik Approach

Second, I reframe. This involved synthesizing insights and reframing what the solution needed to be, not just functional but also manageable.

Visitors bypassed the homepage, relying on direct or saved links. This was due to a lack of proper content design.

I created wireframes and user flows to map content flow and guide users through key information. These were then presented to Departmental leadership teams and iterated based on feedback from admins, students, and marketing.

The page was restructured into a sectional-based architecture, ensuring that updates to sections remain independent of the global layout. This modularity supports long-term scalability and ensures the site remains maintainable for non-technical administrators.

What I Learned

This project required rethinking and refining the homepage to focus on the primary audience: prospective students. Prioritizing a single audience justifies design decisions and disqualifies some, but that is a caveat of design strategy and choosing design direction.
Every homepage component had to justify its existence through user decision-making. For example, eight buttons previously leading to the Research page were consolidated into Two buttons directing users to the Research page and the Industry Opportunities page (which I built). While the inclusion of the latter page primarily benefits the department through sponsorship visibility, I learned that students also perceive it as a signal that the department invests in their success. Even features not directly targeting them can enhance their experience when thoughtfully integrated.
Institutional websites require designing for maintainability as much as for users. Multiple admins with varied responsibilities need to update programs, admissions related details, or course information quickly. For the homepage specifically, the Hero needed to be updated regularly to showcase the newest events and student opportunities the department organized such as Industry seminars and Co-Op Talks. Ensuring ease of updates was a priority to prevent delays that could impact students' education.
