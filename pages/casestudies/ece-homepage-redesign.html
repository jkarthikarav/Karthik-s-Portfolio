ECE Homepage Redesign: Rebuilding Information Flow for Recruitment

Shipped Jan 2025

Role: UI/UX and Web Developer

Employer: ECE Department, University of Toronto

Tools: Figma, WordPress

Summary: Case Study detailing the redesign and WordPress implementation of the University of Toronto ECE department homepage, focused on rebuilding the information hierarchy to serve prospective students.

The ECE department wanted a homepage that could engage with prospective students while accurately reflecting its identity.

Much of the previous content was outdated, some from 2022.
It hard for students to find programs, events, or co-op opportunities.
The homepage needed to be aligned closer with university branding.

I executed the homepage redesign end-to-end: conducting UX research, designing wireframes, prototyping in Figma, presenting iterations to stakeholders, and implementing the final site on WordPress including animations & custom CSS and JS.

Disclaimer: As this public website continues to evolve, the page may differ from the versions shown. I have since left the organization, and any updates made thereafter are at their discretion.

My Process

Unlike other pages I built for the ECE department, such as the Events or Industry Opportunities pages, the homepage was an existing page that required a redesign. My work involved updating and modernizing the structure, improving content visibility, and implementing interactive features.

Designing the Homepage Narrative

I finalized the homepage narrative and identified the key elements needed to tell the story effectively. I focused on creating a Content Architecture that could make the homepage an "Access Hub".

Content Architecture

The sections I designed include:

1. Hero Section → First Impressions
2. Programs → Introduces the various UG & Grad programs
3. Events → To learn about Student Life
4. Research & Industry Opportunities → To target Secondary audiences

The Events section was originally the Hear from the Chair section inherited from the old home page, but the decision to add the events calendar widget was made later (After I built the Events Page).

Each section was built as modular blocks in WordPress, giving the content team the flexibility to update videos, images, cards, CTAs, and widgets independently.

Programs Section

Prospective and current students need an engaging way to explore undergraduate and graduate programs.

Modular cards highlight undergraduate and graduate programs; each card includes an image and a CTA that links directly to the respective program page, encouraging exploration and guiding users to action.
Previously, all stats were presented in simple paragraph and bullet format; the program cards with images and CTAs make information easier to absorb and provide a clear action to the users.

Research and Industry Opportunities Section

Prospective students and industry partners need a clear way to explore research opportunities and potential collaborations.

This was a critical information architecture decision: eliminating eight redundant links and consolidating content.

* Pages I built

Two modular cards highlight Research at ECE and Industry Opportunities*.
Previously, the section included eight research group cards, all linking to the same page, which was redundant.
The new design consolidates information into two cards, providing clear actions.

What I Learned

Unlike pages I built for the department entirely from scratch, this project involved rethinking and refining the information flow. Every component had to earn its place by contributing to that flow and improving decision-making.

Key Takeaway: Everything on a page must matter

A page must function as a purposeful narrative that actively guides users.
