Operationalizing Generative AI: Service Blueprinting for Creative Pipelines

Implemented July 2023

Role: Service Designer & Workflow Architect

Client: Fix It In Post Studios

Methods: Service Blueprinting, Rapid Prototyping, Pilot Implementation

Summary: I developed and validated a "Human-in-the-Loop" AI workflow for a production studio. By integrating Generative AI, we solved the "blank canvas" problem for directors.

Integrating GenAI tools into a rigid production schedule.

In May 2023, Adobe released Generative Fill (in their Beta release). The client, aka my former Boss, saw potential for efficiency but lacked a strategy to deploy it.

The requirement was not just to "use AI," but to design a service layer that made AI reliable & billable. We needed to move from ad-hoc experimentation to a standardized operational workflow within 8 weeks.

Our Problem: Creativity is Subjective

The traditional "Pre-viz" process was ambiguous at best. Directors struggled to articulate their vision, leading to vague briefs, excessive revisions, and friction between creative teams and the Directors.

Ergo: A process where feedback was slow and costly.

Fig 1. The "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

My Strategic Approach

Fully automated workflows were too risky due to the lack of control. Instead, I proposed a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) system.

This approach made AI do all the "heavy lifting" of asset generation (textures, backgrounds, props) while human talent would work on composition, logic, and final polishes. Despite making this decision in 2023, this aligns with recent 2025 findings that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks (MITRIX, 2025).

Process Engineering

I mapped the ecosystem to identify where AI could reduce friction without affecting time and quality. This involved defining roles, conventions, and handoff procedures.

Operational Implementation

A theoretical blueprint is useless without adoption. I facilitated workshops to train the Design team on the new protocols.

The Editor and Client align on tone, eliminating the "guessing game."

The Project Manager/Design Team Lead break the scene into assets that need AI (Backgrounds, Textures) vs. those that needed Humans (Logos, Text, Specific Characters).

While the Sketch Artists worked on the characters, the Designers use Gen-AI to mass-produce assets, refining the best assets through further image manipulation on Photoshop.

The final image is sent for approval, and barring changes, is sent as mutliple passes to the editor for use in their video edit. Sending passes enables further animation on the edit side.

Final Service Blueprint

The resulting document below is the operational manual for the studio. It has been rigorously validated over the pilot program, and barring project-specific requirements, has been used As-Is.

Business Impact

↦ Conceptualization dropped from days to hours, allowing for more within the same budget.
↦ Showing "near-final" visuals earlier significantly reduced client anxiety and last-minute changes.
↦ The workflow naturally resulted in a library of reusable assets, including prompt libraries, decreasing the cost & time of future projects.

What I Learned

This project showed how Service Design is necessary for AI adoption. The technology itself is easily available, but making it useful in a business context requires rigor.

The non-Textbook Service Design Aspect

Service design, primarily taught in Design schools for physical interactions such as Kiosks and CX, was used here for designing internal workflows and shape a service offering.
