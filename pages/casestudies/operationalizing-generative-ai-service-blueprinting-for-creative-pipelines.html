Operationalizing Generative AI for Creative Pipelines [Service Blueprinting]

Implemented July 2023

Summary: I performed service design to develop a Human-in-the-Loop workflow for the studio. By integrating Generative AI, we solved major issues in creative production: Client Anxiety and Last-Minute Changes.

Towards the end of May 2023, Adobe released Generative Fill (in their Beta release).

In June 2023, I was hired to help transition the studio from experimenting with tools to executing it as a service.

The goal of the project was simple: Design a service layer to make GenAI-assisted production billable.

The requirement was to move from ad-hoc AI-prompts to a standardized operational workflow within an 8-week delivery window.

We aimed to move the primary deliverable from a glorified slideshow to an animated Pre-viz with rich visuals.

Our Problem: Creativity is Subjective

The traditional "Pre-viz" process was ambiguous at best. Directors struggled to articulate their vision, leading to vague briefs, excessive revisions, and delivery delays between creative teams and the Directors.

Fig 1. The "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

My Approach

Fully automated workflows were too risky due to the lack of control. Instead, I proposed a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) system.

This approach made AI do all the "heavy lifting" of asset generation (textures, backgrounds, props) while human talent would work on composition, logic, and final polishes. Despite having made this decision in 2023 when AI was breaking through the commercial technology market, this aligns with recent 2025 findings that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks (MITRIX, 2025).

Process Engineering

The core task was designing the infrastructure that allows the studio to function with AI, rather than just using it for one-off tasks.

Designing the Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Framework

I established strict protocols for file formats and layer structures to ensure AI-generated assets remained editable. I also introduced "Alignment Check-ins," ensuring client approval on tone before any destructive design processes began.

Here, we benefitted from generating multiple backgrounds and having our sketch artist design the foreground characters so that we could quickly alternate between different choices with the client. This ensured that they would settle on something instead of endless revision loops.

Fig 1. The "To-Be" workflow where I provided the studio with more control over their output.

Operational Implementation

AKA the Pilot Program

I managed a pilot program to test the new workflow using a live project. This served as a training implementation where I coached the team through the transition to AI generation and compositing.

Communicating Value

Studio could understand and allocate resources effectively.

AI vs Human Work

Taught the team to break scenes into AI-generated assets (BG/Textures) vs. Human-led assets.

Multiple Passes

to deliver assets in layers that the editor could animate.

Workflow explained through the Pilot Program

The Editor and Client align on tone at the beginning.

The Project Manager/Design Team Lead break the scene into assets that need AI (Backgrounds, Textures) vs. those that needed Humans (Logos, Text, Specific Characters).

While the Sketch Artists worked on the characters, the Designers use Gen-AI to mass-produce assets, refining the best assets through further image manipulation on Photoshop.

The final images are sent for approval, and barring changes, is sent as mutliple passes to the editor for use in their video edit. Sending passes enables further animation on the edit side.

Final Blueprint & Impact

The resulting document below is the operational manual for the studio.

It has been rigorously validated over the pilot program, and barring project-specific requirements, has been used As-Is.

Conceptualization dropped from days to hours, allowing for more within the same budget.
Showing these visuals earlier significantly reduced constant changes that employees might percieve as nagging and/or uncertain vision.
The workflow naturally resulted in a library of reusable assets, including prompt libraries, decreasing the cost & time of future projects.

Lessons from Early GenAI Adoption

In 2023, when Generative AI was just emerging, this project showed that AI adoption depends on workflow design. Human-in-the-Loop workflows built team confidence and reduced revision cycles.
Through this, the Studio Increased organizational credibility by getting a head-start on new GenAI tools, resulting in approximately 30–40% revenue growth from repeat projects and client referrals.
We also reduced client anxiety and last-minute tensions massively, improving post-project feedback, employee morale, and reducing project completion times by around two weeks.
