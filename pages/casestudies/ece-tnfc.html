The ECE department had a slowly bubbling up concern: the TNFC lab.

Although the TNFC lab is known to be a department-operated lab, its digital presence still conveyed a lack of connection. Their standalone website did not clearly communicate how the lab fit within the department’s research ecosystem.

This was my final project at ECE. I was responsible for auditing, repurposing, and consolidating TNFC’s content to migrate it into ECE's site.

My Integration Strategy

Before opening Figma, I opened their previous website and a word document in a new tab.

I audited the TNFC website to understand its structure and content. The site had roughly twenty pages and five top-level navigation headings, but much was unnecessarily split across multiple pages due to the WordPress template. Users had to click through subpages to access related information that could have simply been presented together, making it harder to understand the lab’s offerings and access process.

After the audit, I consolidated the content into two pages within the ECE site: a TNFC overview page and a dedicated how to access subpage. Our Marketing team also made the decision to switch the lab's news into the department’s central “Latest News & Insights” section. In my word doc, I rewrote all website content, reorganizing layouts to quickly use pre-existing departmental components and patterns. These patterns were mostly card groups and grids that I'd created during the course of my work at the department. This ensured the lab pages felt like it belonged rather than appearing as a separate, standalone property that was inserted without thought.

Key Findings from the Audit

• Content was unnecessarily divided across multiple pages, fragmenting the user journey.

• Navigation was deep and inconsistent, making it difficult to find key information about access and facilities.

• News and updates about TNFC existed in a separate page, disconnected from overall departmental communications.

• Visual design and layouts did not align with ECE standards.

UI Design

Key UI Decisions - TNFC (Main Page)

• The hero immediately communicates TNFC’s scale, cleanroom environment, and technical sophistication.

• Structured sections (intro, research areas, facility highlights) allow users to quickly understand the lab’s scope and capabilities.

• Sidebar CTAs (“User Access,” “Client Services,” “Contact TNFC”) guide users toward actionable next steps.

• Including contact details for faculty and technical staff supports engagement.

The Initially Approved mockup.

Key UI Decisions - TNFC (Access Page)

• Clear listing of mandatory forms and training ensures users understand compliance requirements upfront.

• Financial and billing information is presented concisely, with discounts clearly communicated for qualifying researchers.

• Quick links to PDF forms (instead of hyperlinks in the original) make essential resources easy to find.

Web Development

I built the TNFC pages directly in WordPress while adhering to the University’s brand and accessibility guidelines. Components were designed to be modular, allowing marketing or web production teams (at the time, also me) to update images, PDFs, or text without breaking layouts or requiring developer intervention.

Why This Page Mattered

Some would say I designed a webpage for a research lab, but this was so much more.
TNFC had historically existed as an independent digital entity, and its website reflected that independence: over twenty pages spread critical information across multiple subpages, forcing users to click through to find what mattered. In 2025, it was essential that its digital presence finally told a story that aligned with the ECE department.
I was given this project in February 2025, and by the first week of April, the pages were live. No fancy focus groups, no surveys: just careful content auditing and stakeholder workshopping. I determined what content was essential and what could be consolidated. By reducing more than twenty pages to just two core pages, I was able to tell more with less. Each section, from key research areas to facility highlights and access requirements, was positioned strategically to communicate TNFC’s scale without overwhelming the visitor.
Not every UX process is necessary for every project. Here, every decision was informed by compliance and regulation: user access, mandatory training, billing, and safety protocols had to be accurate and actionable. At the same time, the pages needed to feel like a natural part of ECE, visually and structurally, while letting TNFC’s value shine. The result is the lab’s story: positioning TNFC as an integral, high-value research facility/laboratory within the department.
