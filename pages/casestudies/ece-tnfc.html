Website Migration and Content Design

Migrating Toronto Nanofabrication Centre from its standalone website into the ECE site

—

The Toronto Nanofabrication Centre (TNFC) reopened in November 2023 after a renovation, but its website was outdated and existed separately of ECE at U of T. Originally working on Content and UI standardisation on the ECE website, I was asked to assess options for the TNFC website.

My role was Web Design and Development

I was responsible for auditing and designing the content architecture, designing and obtaining stakeholder approval from both TNFC and ECE at U of T, and developing the website inside ECE's WP environment. I submitted my research and design proposal in November 2024, got the green light to build in February 2025, and shipped in late March 2025. This is the page I built: Toronto Nanofabrication Centre .

Disclaimer: As this public website continues to evolve, the page may differ from the versions shown. I have since left the organization, and any updates made thereafter are at their discretion.

TNFC's website had four top-level navigation items - User Access being one of them - with auto-generated landing pages for each top-level item with hyperlinked text to subpages. Only User Access needed a dedicated page. The other three could live on the home page or User Access page as concise grid- or card-based sections.

What were the Stakeholder's Key Priorities?

These were identified through stakeholder alignment, and were the criterion upon which the site's worth would be judged.

1

Clear Lab Identity: Position TNFC clearly within ECE, showing it as an integral research facility rather than a standalone lab.

2

Easy Access: How could users quickly find usage policies, educational resources, and regulations?

3

Content & Design: Asked to prioritize content rewrites and layouts to match ECE’s design.

I opted for performance and convenience for the presenter. Every cut was a feasibility AND design decision.

What did I achieve with this migration?

This migration required building with scalability in mind. THe pages I proposed were structured using reusable components and patterns that could be extracted and applied beyond TNFC.
The impact of that architectural work became clear after my departure from the organization (This was my last project for ECE). The Grid Modernization Centre was developed using the same hero banner and page patterns originally designed for TNFC. This reuse, I believe, validates the robustness of my designs.

What I Learned

This was not just a webpage for a research lab. This project was about designing language and structure that made the process easier and reduced administrative work.
TNFC had previously existed as a separate digital entity; Not anymore. Its website reflected this with its own independent news and blog feed. The pages I built went live by the end of March; User Access in early April.
Every design decision was driven by compliance and regulation, including access, training, billing, and safety. At the same time, the pages were designed to feel native to ECE while clearly articulating TNFC’s value. The result positions TNFC as a core, high-value research facility within the department.
