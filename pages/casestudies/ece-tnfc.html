The Toronto NanoFabrication Centre (TNFC)

The Karthik Approach

This case study focuses on how I consolidated TNFC lab's standalone website into a two-page section within the ECE site, improving discoverability and reducing site management overhead.

Context

—

The Toronto Nanofabrication Centre (TNFC) reopened in November 2023 after a renovation, but its separately-run website was still outdated. When ECE at U of T's Admin reviewed digital priorities a year later, the site stood out as a problem.

I had been working on content updates and UI standardisation across the ECE website, so I was also assigned to research the TNFC site and assess options.

I submitted my proposal in November 2024, got the green light in February 2025, and shipped in late March 2025, after wrapping up higher-priority ECE website work first. This is the page I built: Toronto Nanofabrication Centre .

Disclaimer: As this public website continues to evolve, the page may differ from the versions shown. I have since left the organization, and any updates made thereafter are at their discretion.

UX Research: Nov 2024 – Dec 2025

Design & Front-End Dev: Mar 2025

Content Audit & Restructuring

—

TNFC's website had five top-level navigation items - User Access being one of them - with auto-generated landing pages for each top-level item with hyperlinked text to subpages. Only User Access needed a dedicated page. The other four could live on the home page or User Access page as concise grid- or card-based sections.

The original design used long paragraphs to explain access requirements, with easy-to-miss mandatory steps such as agreements, forms, training videos, etc, buried as hyperlinks in body text.

Key Priorities

—

These were identified through stakeholder alignment, and were the criterion upon which the site's worth would be judged.

1

Clear Lab Identity: Position TNFC clearly within ECE, showing it as an integral research facility rather than a standalone lab.

2

Easy Access: How could users quickly find usage policies, educational resources, and regulations?

3

Content & Design: Were critical. Asked to prioritize content rewrites and layouts to match ECE’s design.

Designing the new UI

—

I designed structured layouts and a simplified information architecture to guide user actions.

The homepage explains what TNFC offers, with content from the original's top-level pages condensed into structured sections. The Access page lists all required forms and processes, with related documents available.

Exceeding Stakeholder Expectations

—

The transition from design to development required building with scalability in mind. Pages were structured using reusable components and patterns that could be extracted and applied beyond TNFC.

The impact of that architectural work became clear after my departure from the organization (This was my last project for ECE).

The Grid Modernization Centre was developed using the same hero banner and page patterns originally designed for TNFC. This reuse, I believe, validates the robustness of my designs.

What I Learned

—

This was not just a webpage for a research lab. This project was about designing language and structure that made the process easier and reduced administrative work.

TNFC had previously existed as a separate digital entity; Not anymore. Its website reflected this with its own independent news and blog feed. The pages I built went live by the end of March; User Access in early April.

Every design decision was driven by compliance and regulation, including access, training, billing, and safety. At the same time, the pages were designed to feel native to ECE while clearly articulating TNFC’s value. The result positions TNFC as a core, high-value research facility within the department.
