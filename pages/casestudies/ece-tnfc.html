The Context

A lab reopening without a modernized digital presence.

The TNFC lab had reopened, but its website hadn't evolved with it.

The Toronto Nanofabrication Centre underwent a renovation and reopened in November 2023. Despite this renewal, in February 2025, its digital presence remained outdated and disconnected from the ECE website & intranet.

Information was split-up across multiple pages, requirements were buried in hyperlinks and academic language, and the site operated in isolation rather than as an integrated part of ECE's digital presence.

The primary task was restructuring and rewriting existing content to modernize the digital experience.

This is the page I built: Toronto Nanofabrication Centre .

— What Stakeholders Wanted

Key priorities identified through stakeholder alignment:

Clear Lab Identity

Position TNFC clearly within ECE, showing it as an integral research facility rather than a standalone lab.

Easy Access

How could users quickly find usage policies, educational resources, and regulations?

Content & Design

Were critical. Asked to prioritize content rewrites and layouts to match ECE’s design.

Content Audit & Restructuring

Mapped fifteen fragmented pages into a consolidated two-page structure.

The original TNFC website contained roughly fifteen pages across five top-level navigation items. A full content audit revealed the pattern.

Users had to click repeatedly just to understand how to get started. Below are the original pages:

I recognized that content was written as long-form narrative and unnecessarily divided. Important compliance actions were buried within hyperlinked text rather than highlighted as Calls-to-action for users to take.

We recognize that research validation doesn't guarantee real-world success when you change two variables at once.

UI Design

Designed structured layouts to surface compliance requirements and guide user actions.

The original design relied on descriptive paragraphs to communicate user access requirements. Mandatory steps like agreements, forms, and training videos, were embedded as hyperlinks within body text.

This meant users could easily overlook what was actually required of them before they could use the lab. View my mockups below:

The redesigned pages used intentional layout hierarchy to prioritize user needs. The TNFC homepage hero immediately communicates scale, with structured sections (intro, research areas, facility highlights) allowing users to quickly understand scope.

The Access page listed mandatory forms and related documents as clickable buttons that opened PDFs.

Front-End Development & Design-to-Dev

Built with modular architecture to scale across ECE's public website.

The transition from design to development required building with scalability in mind. Pages were structured using reusable components and patterns that could be extracted and applied beyond TNFC.

The impact of that architectural work became clear after my departure from the organization (This was my last project for ECE). The Grid Modernization Centre was developed using the same hero banner and page patterns originally designed for TNFC.

The reuse validates the robustness of my designs.

Why This Page Mattered

This was not just a webpage for a research lab. This project was about designing language and structure that made the process easier and reduced administrative work.
TNFC had previously existed as a separate digital entity; Not anymore. Its website reflected this with its own independent news and blog feed. The pages I built went live by the end of March; User Access in early April.
Every design decision was driven by compliance and regulation, including access, training, billing, and safety. At the same time, the pages were designed to feel native to ECE while clearly articulating TNFC’s value. The result positions TNFC as a core, high-value research facility within the department.
