IA Redesign and Navigation Menu testing Strategy Design

For the Textile Museum of Canada
Tasked with fixing the IA, I found that changing the navigation menu design would make it hard to isolate impact, so I presented testing them in phases.

Academic Project Oct – Dec 2023 • Role: UX Researcher (Team of 3) • IA + Mockups using Figma, Miro, Optimal Workshop

Project Context

This was a Year 1 graduate level project for a real client (Textile Museum of Canada) aimed to improve the discoverability of content and wayfinding across their website. Therefore, our research focused on understanding user needs and behaviors related to how visitors find and navigate information on the site.

Research Process

Content Inventory

Audited existing pages and menu items to identify redundancies and unclear labeling.

Card Sorting & Tree Testing

Conducted card sorting with users to understand their mental models. Used tree testing (Optimal Workshop) to validate navigation paths.

Competitive Benchmarking

Reviewed navigation and IA patterns from leading museum and arts websites.

Key Findings

Overlapping categories and vague menu labels

Struggled to locate subpages

Hamburger menu obscured navigation

Accessibility issues with color contrast

Test Strategy Design and Reasoning

While the card sort and tree testing were done by the entire team, I personally created the visual navigation menus, informed by our research. Unlike many school projects, where changes to information architecture and navigation are combined and assessed together, I deliberately separated these.

While aiming to isolate cause and effect, I could get genuinely actionable next steps for stakeholders. I was asked by my team, "Why is such a testing/implementation strategy even needed for an IA redesign?". Simple, Isolating the impact of each change is essential in making positive progress and satisfying client goals.

By rolling out the new information architecture separately from the visual navigation redesign, we can attribute any improvements in user task success, discoverability, or satisfaction to the correct intervention.

This approach, while more complex, prevents wasted resources, builds client trust, and ensures that future design decisions are always supported by the proper data. Although not required in a school project, it’s vital for real-world implementations.

I realized that changing both the IA and the navigation menu design at once would make it difficult to isolate the impact of each. To ensure we could measure the effect of the new IA independently, I proposed a phased implementation and testing strategy:

Phase 1: Test New IA in Existing Menu

Integrate the new, consolidated IA into the current menu structure with minimal visual changes. Gather user feedback and analytics to assess improvements in navigation and content discoverability.

Phase 2: Test New Navigation Menu Design

Move away from the hamburger menu to a visible, mega menu-styled navigation bar. Gather user feedback and analytics to assess improvements in navigation and content discoverability.

Rationale behind Two Phase Strategy

Clearly attribute improvements to the correct intervention. Reduce disruption to real users. Ensure changes are real-time data-driven.

What I Designed

Phase 1:

The short-term solution that will integrate the new consolidated information architecture into the existing navigation menu design. This will allow testing with real users and gathering metrics, while avoiding disruption from a complete overhaul of the navigation system. It will also address accessibility issues, as the primary and secondary colors lack sufficient contrast.​

Phase 2:

The long-term solution that brings the menu out of the hamburger. This approach also allowed for more flexible use of color, to improve the design using the WCAG contrast and accessibility guidelines.

What I Learned

The goal of this project was to create a clear, accessible user experience that makes it easier for people to discover and navigate the Textile Museum of Canada’s rich collections, exhibitions, and events.

This project also strengthened my ability to prioritize features and content, focusing on what would have the most impact for both first-time visitors and returning patrons. I found that building trust online comes from making the digital experience simple and transparent.

Key Takeaway: Blending User Needs with Post-deployment Business Goals

The most valuable insight was learning to balance immediate user needs such as content discoverability with the museum’s long-term goals, such as increasing engagement and supporting their growth.

Key Takeaway: Phased, Research-driven Planning

Implementing a phased plan ensured that both user needs and organizational goals were addressed, allowing measurable improvements to be clearly attributed to the correct changes.
