The Challenge

Fix It In Post Studios (FIIP Studios) wondered: "What was Post-Production going to look like in the AI era?"

Between 2022-23, the release of ChatGPT, Midjourney v4, and Firefly triggered experimentation across companies and anxiety over employability in the future.

In 2022, Indian startups couldn't afford the effort or time to build their own in-house AI tool, and many wanted quick wins over ethics, implementation guidelines, and training.

GenAI had many caveats: Designers were uncertain about relevance, AI outputs were inconsistent and unstable, and Clients were unclear about value.

However, If the AI hype and marketing failed, startups would have burned too much money and would be left unable to compete for human talent in the future.

Being a relatively young startup, FIIP Studios wanted to use this chaos to set themselves apart. They did not want a quick win, they wanted a plan.

Focal Question

How should a video productions solutions studio respond to generative AI without compromising on output quality or human-led creative control?

Framework Design

For integrating generative AI tools into workflows in both standard and case-by-case scenarios.

Client Needs

To protect creative control and integrity by defining methods and oversight processes to maintain output quality.

Validation

Of the framework or workflows generated through framework in a pilot project to measure real-time effectiveness.

Karthik's Systems Thinking

This phase involved mapping genAI tool evolution, analyzing market signals and limitations to define guidelines for workflow integration.

One Strong Idea emerged from applying the design guidelines.

Use AI inside structured workflows to mass produce swappable visual assets during early-stage exploration. Our Mass production idea ensures that designers still had to evaluate AI outputs to ensure quality and usability.

Even 2 years after this decision, studies by other researchers and companies show that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks.

— The Design Guidelines

After my research, we had these Design Principles

AI as copilot-style infrastructure

AI can be built into existing processes to increase speed of output, but is not at the level required to replace entire creative teams.

Quantity, not Quality

Teams need to generate options quickly because stakeholders require options and can accept that visual polish comes later.

Focus on specific phases not entirety

AI performs well in specific phases such as concept generation, ideation, and mockup generation.

Changes should not require starting over

Most AI Tools have difficulty swapping specific sections or generating alternatives without rebuilding entire outputs.

What is a Pre-Viz?

A movie director approached Fix It In Post Studios to create an animated Pre-viz video to help market to potential investors. A Pre-Viz is movie jargon for a video used to align producers and crew before committing. The irony isn't lost on me.

Firstly, I studied the Current "As-Is" Workflow.

The traditional Pre-viz creation process is what we would call a "bottleneck."

It kept the studio in a reactive, freelance-dependent environment, where the studio couldn't move until the Director did the heavy lifting.

Fig 1. "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

— Key Pain Points

The Current Workflow had these issues:

If a Director couldn't articulate their vision, the studio remained idle.

Without a clear division between environment and character assets, any creative change required a massive restart.

Vague briefs and long silences led to excessive last-minute changes.

The Karthik Approach

A standard Pre-Viz was a well-established project type with existing workflows. Therefore, my new workflow couldn't diverge from existing mental models by too much.

To ease all stakeholder concerns, I used the guidelines to propose a copilot-style workflow.

This approach makes AI do the menial tasks (generating backgrounds, props, etc) while the designers work on composition and visual polish.

Even though Photoshop's Generative Fill feature was BETA-launched two weeks prior, we were prepared for by tasking Designers with prompt structures & stylistic goals based on the discovery session.

Fig 2. "To-Be" workflow where the studio had more control over the output.

I intentionally made sure that we shifted the technical burden from the Director to the Studio (applying Guideline 4), made decision-making faster by allowing quick choice alternation (applying Guideline 2), and enabled studio to vet AI outputs before any destructive processes began (applying Guideline 1).

The pilot project validated that AI when embedded structurally, could reduce bottlenecks without compromising creative ownership. Each phase was scoped by applying Guideline 3; AI was assigned only to specific tasks rather than the entire pipeline.

Workflow Implementation

I managed the pilot program to test the new workflow using a live project, ensuring the copilot-style logic worked in a high-pressure environment.

Tone Alignment

The Creative Director and Client align on tone at the beginning.

Visual reference used to establish creative direction and expectations.

Scene Breakdown

The Project Manager and Design Lead break the scene into assets that require AI generation, such as backgrounds and textures, versus assets requiring human detailing, such as logos, text, and main characters.

Layer logic defined before asset production begins.

Designing with AI

While sketch artists worked on character development, junior designers used generative AI to mass-produce environmental assets, refining select outputs through Photoshop.

AI-generated backgrounds refined for production use.

QA, Approval, Compositing, Pass to Edit

Final images are sent for approval and exported as layered passes for editing. Delivering separate passes enables downstream animation and motion control during the edit.

Layered exports prepared for motion and compositing.

The Final Blueprint

Finally, I achieved FIIP Studios' goal of making GenAI-assisted production billable, while also future-proofing.

The resulting blueprint shifted the studio from a reactive workflow to a proactive service.

By building the backstage, including structured GenAI prompts, QA checkpoints, and task/sub-task boundaries, the studio gained control over output quality and delivery without undermining morale or creative ownership.

The blueprint has been rigorously validated over the pilot program, and barring project-specific requirements, is used As-Is.

What I Learned

In 2022-23, All of us were bombarded with two distinct opinions/approaches regarding AI implementation.
One was that it would replace designers en masse.
The other was that AI could handle labour-intensive tasks done by junior designers, ensuring a business only needed senior designers who would focus on “high-value” work.
These oversimplified AI approaches erase critical labour and torpedoes traditional avenues for mentorship and workforce development. In reality, every task is crucial, even naming files.
I love working with innovative founders who don't look at AI as a band aid but as a tool. In this case study, junior designers and interns were tasked with generating using AI, so that they would learn critical and creative design thinking as opposed to learning design tools.
As a result, the studio strengthened their own credibility through early, structured AI adoption.
