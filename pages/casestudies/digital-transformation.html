The Challenge

What was the future of Film Production under AI-enabled technological disruption?

In late 2022, AI tools rapidly entered creative workflows. Over the following year, the release of ChatGPT, Midjourney v4, Stable Diffusion, Adobe Firefly, and Generative Fill triggered both experimentation and widespread anxiety across design teams.

At this stage, none of the studios in India could afford the cost, effort, or time to build their own in-House AI tool, and quick chats with Clients revealed they'd want to see the above AI tools used to set their outputs apart from their competitors.

Being a relatively young studio, FIIP studios attempted to use this chaos to set themselves apart.

There were other issues though:

• Designers were uncertain about long-term relevance.

• AI outputs were inconsistent and stylistically unstable.

• Clients were curious but unclear about value.

Focal Question

How should a video productions solutions studio respond to generative AI advances without compromising creative control, team morale, or output quality?

Objectives

1

To design a framework for integrating generative AI tools into studio workflows relevant in both standard and case-by-case scenarios.

2

Client Needs

To protect creative control and integrity by defining necessity, methods, and oversight to maintain output quality and team morale.

3

To validate the framework or workflows generated through framework in a real-time project to measure real-time effectiveness.

Diagnosing

Technology Landscaping

Mapped evolution of generative tools (2022–2023), analyzing market signals and limitations to help define design guidelines.

Between November 2022 and May 2023, generative AI moved from experimentation to workflow-level adoption across creative industries.

Crucially, If the hype from AI companies and marketers failed to materialize at scale, the studio could not afford to lose its current skilled human talent. Rehiring skilled talent and deprioritizing is not cost-effective in the long run, especially for a smaller scale studio.

Capability Evolution & Timeline

• November 2022: Midjourney v4 alpha enabled high-quality, cinematic concept art and style frames; became the default tool for early 2023.

• Late 2022: Stable Diffusion matured for photorealistic environments, textures, and matte paintings; weak in character continuity and animation.

• Nov 2022 onward: ChatGPT widely used for script ideation, storyboarding, and copy drafts; effective for rapid ideation but weak at long-form narrative and originality.

• March–May 2023: Adobe Firefly and Photoshop Generative Fill introduced IP-safe, enterprise-ready image generation and non-destructive editing into professional stacks.

Adoption Patterns by Sub-Industry

• Film & TV: Pre-viz, concept art, pitch decks; adoption mostly experimental within art teams.

• Animation: Backgrounds, environment concepts, narrative ideation; low-to-moderate adoption.

• Game Development: Environment concepts, stylized textures; moderate adoption; limited character pipeline use.

• Advertising & Marketing: Visual concepts, key visuals, copy drafts; moderate adoption, mostly internal/low-risk.

• Design Studios & Photography: Moodboards, style directions, UI explorations, Photoshop compositing; early-phase experimentation.

Market Signals & Tensions

• Buzz from Midjourney and ChatGPT contrasted with adoption via Adobe Creative Cloud.

• Anxiety about creative displacement

• Human oversight needed for quality and ethical safeguards.

Insights to Guidelines

Converted market research into guidelines for how the studio should integrate GenAI into their workflows for service offerings.

During the Define stage, several ideas emerged about integrating AI into existing workflows. One stood out: Use AI inside structured workflows to mass produce swappable visual assets during early-stage exploration.

Mass production was merely a strategy that AI could help with; designers still had to evaluate and ensure the quality and usability of these mass produced designs. This aligns with recent 2025 findings that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks (MITRIX, 2025).

Design Principles

1

AI as workflow infrastructure

Core Insight Behind the Statement: AI can be built into existing processes to increase speed of output, but is not at the level required to replace entire creative teams.

2

Quantity not Quality

Core Insight Behind the Statement: Teams need to generate options quickly because stakeholders require options and can accept that visual polish comes later.

3

Changes should not require starting over

Core Insight Behind the Statement: Most AI Tools have difficulty swapping specific sections or generating alternatives without rebuilding entire outputs.

4

Focus on specific phases not entirety

Core Insight Behind the Statement: AI performs well in specific phases such as concept generation, ideation, and mockup generation.

Towards the end of May 2023, Adobe released Generative Fill (in their Beta release). Now, we moved from conceptual discussions to practically usable workflows. The Studio wanted to move from ad-hoc AI-prompts to a standardized operational workflow within an 8-week delivery window.

Designing

The Opportunity

In June 2023, A movie director wanted an animated Pre-viz video.

A Pre-Viz is used to align producers and other crew members before committing to full production. This made it a suitable project to test AI-assisted asset generation under human control.

I was asked to design a service layer to make GenAI-assisted production billable, in particular, using this project as a live implementation case.

This was not a unique project at the outset. Earlier when I used to be a full time employee, I'd worked on similar projects with other directors. That meant I knew the existing workflow, as did every other employee at the studio.

The Existing Workflow

The traditional Pre-viz creation process is what AI would call an "operational bottleneck."

It kept the studio in an reactive/freelance collaboration-esque environment, as seen in the As-Is workflow, where the studio couldn't move until the Director did the heavy lifting.

Key Pain Points:

• If a Director couldn't articulate their vision, the studio remained idle.

• Without a clear division between environment and character assets, any creative change required a massive restart.

• Vague briefs and long silences led to excessive last-minute changes.

Fig 1. "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

The New Workflow

Fully automated workflows were risky due to the lack of control. Therefore, I proposed a copilot-style workflow.

Even though the AI feature in Photoshop was literally BETA-launched two weeks before we started, we were prepared for the randomness with which AI generated lighting, style, and color that varies wildly from one output to the next.

We (the operational Studio Head and I) solved that by tasking the Graphic Designers with strict prompt structures and a pre-confirmed post-generation color grading to lock-in the visual identity.

This approach made AI do all the "heavy lifting" of asset generation (textures, backgrounds, props) while human talent would work on decision-making, composition, logic, and final polishes. The core task was designing the infrastructure that allows the studio to function with AI, rather than just using it for one-off tasks.

Fig 2. "To-Be" workflow where the studio had more control over the output.

Key Design Decisions

Shifting the Technical Burden

from the Director to the Studio; Established protocols for file formats and layer structures.

Faster Decision Making

Alternating between choices quickly, forcing decisions and reducing revision loops.

Safety & Quality Control

by enabling studio to vet AI outputs before any destructive design processes began.

Stress Test

Workflow Implementation

I managed the pilot program to test the new workflow using a live project, ensuring the copilot-style logic worked in a high-pressure environment.

The Stress-Test validated that AI, when embedded inside a structured workflow, could reduce bottlenecks in Pre-viz without compromising creative ownership.

Tone Alignment

The Editor and Client align on tone at the beginning.

Visual reference used to establish creative direction and expectations.

Scene Breakdown

The Project Manager and Design Lead break the scene into assets that require AI generation, such as backgrounds and textures, versus assets requiring human precision, such as logos, text, and specific characters.

Layer logic defined before asset production begins.

Designing with AI

While sketch artists worked on character development, designers used generative AI to mass-produce environmental assets, refining selected outputs through image manipulation in Photoshop.

AI-generated backgrounds refined for production use.

QA, Approval, Compositing, Pass to Edit

Final images are sent for approval and, once validated, exported as layered passes to the editor. Delivering separate passes enables downstream animation and motion control within the edit.

Layered exports prepared for motion and compositing.

Final Blueprint

The resulting document is essentially an operational manual that shifted the studio from a reactive, client action-dependent model to a proactive, studio-led service structure.

By building the backstage, including structured GenAI prompts, QA checkpoints, and task/sub-task boundaries, the studio gained control over output quality and delivery. Importantly, the system was designed to integrate AI into existing workflows without undermining morale or creative ownership.

The following blueprint has been rigorously validated over the pilot program, and barring project-specific requirements, has been used As-Is.

Lessons from Early GenAI Adoption

In 2023, as Generative AI entered the commercial market, this project shows that successful adoption depends less on the AI itself and more on workflow design. The project demonstrated that a copilot-style approach, where the human remains the pilot/driving force, was more effective than full automation.
Believing AI-use in the studio was to replace designers en masse would have been a reactionary approach to online fear mongering. Equally misleading was the PR that AI was built to handle labor-intensive tasks done by junior designers, freeing senior designers to focus on “high-value” work. These oversimplified AI approaches erases critical labor and torpedoes traditional avenues for mentorship and workforce development. In reality, every design task is crucial, even generating background images.
This live implementation accelerated organizational learning and surfaced technical pain points that a theoretical blueprint would have missed. By acting as both Service Designer and Pilot Project Manager, I was able to iterate the workflow in real time and translate principles into team-level systems. Most importantly, Core creative direction remained human-led.
As a result, the studio strengthened their own credibility through early, structured AI adoption, achieving approximately 30–40% revenue growth from repeat projects and referrals, reducing project timelines by around two weeks, and significantly lowering client anxiety and last-minute tensions while improving employee morale.
