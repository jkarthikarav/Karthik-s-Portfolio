The Challenge

The Bosses at Fix It In Post Studios wondered: "What was the future of Film Production under AI-enabled technological disruption?"

In late 2022, AI tools rapidly entered creative workflows.

Between 2022-23, the release of ChatGPT, Midjourney v4, and Firefly triggered experimentation and anxiety across teams.

At this stage, Indian studios couldn't afford the effort, or time to build their own in-House AI tool, and their clients wanted quick integrations to offset production costs.

There were issues with genAI though: Designers were uncertain about long-term relevance, AI outputs were inconsistent and stylistically unstable, and Clients were curious but unclear about value.

Being a relatively young studio, FIIP studios attempted to use this chaos to set themselves apart.

— Focal Question

How should a video productions solutions studio respond to generative AI advances without compromising creative control, team morale, or output quality?

Project Objectives

Framework Design

To design a framework for integrating generative AI tools into studio workflows relevant in both standard and case-by-case scenarios.

Client Needs

To protect creative control and integrity by defining necessity, methods, and oversight to maintain output quality and team morale.

Validation

To validate the framework or workflows generated through framework in a real-time project to measure real-time effectiveness.

Karthik's Systems Thinking

This project involved mapping the evolution of genAI tools, analyzing market signals and limitations to help define design guidelines for integrating GenAI into studio workflows.

Between November 2022 and May 2023, generative AI moved from experimentation to workflow-level adoption across creative industries.

If the hype from AI companies and marketers failed to materialize at scale, the studio could not afford to lose its current skilled human talent.

One Idea emerged from the guidelines: Use AI inside structured workflows to mass produce swappable visual assets during early-stage exploration.

Mass production was merely a strategy that AI could help with as designers still had to evaluate and ensure quality and usability. This aligns with recent 2025 findings that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks [Source].

— The Design Guidelines

After my research, we had these Design Principles

AI as copilot-style infrastructure

AI can be built into existing processes to increase speed of output, but is not at the level required to replace entire creative teams.

Quantity not Quality

Teams need to generate options quickly because stakeholders require options and can accept that visual polish comes later.

Focus on specific phases not entirety

AI performs well in specific phases such as concept generation, ideation, and mockup generation.

Changes should not require starting over

Most AI Tools have difficulty swapping specific sections or generating alternatives without rebuilding entire outputs.

Lessons from Early GenAI Adoption

Believing AI-use in the studio was to replace designers en masse would have been a reactionary approach to online fear mongering. Equally misleading was the PR that AI was built to handle labor-intensive tasks done by junior designers, freeing senior designers to focus on “high-value” work. These oversimplified AI approaches erases critical labor and torpedoes traditional avenues for mentorship and workforce development. In reality, every task is crucial, even naming files.
Working together with innovative founders who are not looking at AI as a band aid solution but as a toll that aids their long-term goals is something I always do, this project being an example of that. I am excited to see how the workflows developed as a result of these design guidelines evolve.
