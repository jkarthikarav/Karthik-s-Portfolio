The Challenge

Fix It In Post Studios (FIIP Studios) wondered: "What was Post-Production going to look like in the genAI era?"

Breaking Through AI Anxiety in a Fast-Moving Industry

Fix It In Post Studios, an Indian video production solutions startup, faced a defining question during the 2022–23 wave of generative AI disruption: how to respond to genAI tools like ChatGPT, Midjourney, and Adobe Firefly without compromising output quality or undermining the creative teams.

While competitors chased quick wins, FIIP Studios wanted a principled plan that would set them apart rather than leave them overexposed when the hype faded.

Reduced revision loops and increased morale regarding AI implementation.

The Karthik Approach

A Framework-First Strategy for Responsible AI Adoption

Rather than treating AI as a tool rollout, I approached the challenge as a service design problem. Through structured research and workflow analysis, I developed four core design guidelines and validated them through a live pilot project.

— The Design Guidelines

I delivered these Design Guidelines

AI as copilot-style infrastructure

AI to be embedded into existing workflows to accelerate output speed, operating as infrastructure.

Quantity, not Quality

Prioritize rapid generation of options during early-stages, where stakeholders require volume, not finished assets.

Focus on specific phases not entirety

AI can be scoped to specific tasks rather than applied across the entire pipeline.

Changes should not require starting over

Workflows will be redesigned to eliminate swapping/alternative generation difficulties without having to rebuild entire outputs.

One strong idea emerged from applying the design guidelines.

Use AI inside structured workflows to mass produce swappable visual assets during early-stage exploration. Our Mass production idea ensures that designers still had to evaluate AI outputs to ensure quality and usability.

Even 2 years after this decision, studies by other researchers and companies show that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks.

In May 2023, a movie director approached FIIP Studios to create an animated Pre-viz video to help align producers and crew before committing to production.

Designing the Workflow as a Service Problem, Not a Tool Rollout

Understanding the As-Is System

I was asked to create a concrete, billable service validated against a live client project.

Firstly, I studied the Current "As-Is" Workflow.

The traditional pre-visualization workflow had a structural flaw: the Director was the center of every decision, making the studio dependent on their ability to articulate.

When a Director couldn't articulate their vision, the studio sat idle or worse, made progress down the wrong aisle.

Without understanding the studio's technical capabilities, creative changes might need a near-complete restart.

And vague briefs compounded over time, becoming excessive last-minute revisions that devastate timelines and trust.

Fig 1. "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

Redesigning the Service

A standard Pre-Viz was a well-established project type with existing workflows. Therefore, my new workflow couldn't diverge from existing mental models by too much.

My workflow for this Pre-Viz project made AI do the menial tasks (generating backgrounds, props, etc) while the designers worked on composition and visual polish.

Even though Photoshop's Generative Fill feature was BETA-launched two weeks prior, the team was prepared.

I intentionally made sure that the studio shifted the technical burden to the Studio (Guideline 4), made decision-making faster by allowing alternation (Guideline 2), and enabled studio to vet AI outputs before polish began (Guideline 1).

Fig 2. "To-Be" workflow where the studio had more control over the output.

The pilot confirmed that the redesigned workflow held under real production pressure (Guideline 3). The studio gained meaningful control over pacing, output quality, and client communication without increasing senior designer workload.

— Workflow Implementation

How did the Blueprint unfold in real time?

Tone Alignment

Rather than waiting for a full brief, the workflow opened with a facilitated alignment session.

Scene Breakdown

The Project Manager and Design Lead decomposed each scene into assets that needed AI or Human before touching any tools.

Designing with AI

Junior designers mass-produced environmental assets using Generative Fill while sketch artists developed core characters in parallel.

QA, Approval, Compositing, Pass to Edit

Final assets were reviewed against the established creative direction, then exported as layered passes to give the edit team flexibility for animation and VFX.

Overall, I helped shift the studio from reactive workflows to having proactive services.

The blueprint, including structured prompt libraries, QA checkpoints, and clearly defined task boundaries, was adopted as the studio's standard operating model.

The service wasn't just experimental GenAI-assisted production, but it was now billable and repeatable.

What I Learned

This case study is to show how a structured, principles-driven approach to AI adoption can transform creative operations without sacrificing human-centred design principles or output quality for speed and cost.
Oversimplified AI approaches often erase critical labour and torpedoes traditional avenues for mentorship and workforce development. In reality, every task is crucial, even that of naming files. In the live pilot project, Junior designers were tasked with generating using AI. This was so that they would learn critical and creative design thinking as opposed to simply learning design tools.
As a result, the studio strengthened their own credibility through early, structured AI adoption.
