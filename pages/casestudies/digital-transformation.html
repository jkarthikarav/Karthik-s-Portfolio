The Opportunity

—

Fall 2022, OpenAI, Midjourney, and Adobe had burst onto the scene, and every client wanted agencies to use them. So, FIIP Studios wanted a plan that would set them apart rather than leave them vulnerable when the hype faded. There were other factors to consider: whether design roles would survive or would adoption risk destroying credibility.

How did I Study Designers, Workflows, and Generative AI tools? Ethnographic and Observational studies

What did I Create? A Framework with 4 design guidelines to justify generative AI choices.

What was The Framework?

1

Copilot-style infrastructure: AI to be embedded into existing workflows to accelerate output speed, operating as infrastructure.

2

Quantity, not Quality: Prioritize rapid generation of options during early-stages, where stakeholders require volume, not finished assets.

3

Focus on specific phases: AI can be scoped to specific tasks rather than applied across the entire pipeline.

4

Changes without restarting: Workflows will be redesigned to eliminate inherent issues with AI generation (having to rebuild entire outputs).

One strong idea emerged from applying the design guidelines. Use AI inside structured workflows to mass produce swappable visual assets during early-stage exploration.

The Opportunity

—

In May 2023, a movie director approached FIIP Studios to create an animated Pre-viz video to help align his producers and crew to commit to his film's production.

What is a Pre Viz? A Pre-Viz is a video used to pilot concepts and ideas before production. Storyboarding in Video.

Why a Pre Viz? Irony. To test out concepts from our AI adoption framework to deliver a concept-testing artifact.

Process Audit

—

The traditional pre-visualization workflow had a structural flaw: the Director was the center of every decision, making the studio, or as was the norm, a single video editor, dependent on the director's ability to articulate.

When a Director couldn't articulate their vision, the studio sat idle or worse, made progress down the wrong aisle. Without understanding the studio's technical capabilities, creative changes might need a near-complete restart. And vague briefs compounded over time, becoming excessive last-minute revisions that devastate timelines and trust.

Fig 1. "As-Is" workflow: Every decision gate required Director input; so every delay was a Director delay.

Fixing the workflow wasn't simply adding an AI tool. It required Directors to give something up.

Future State Design

—

I proposed a blueprint where I intentionally shifted the technical burden to the Studio (Guideline 4), made decision-making faster by allowing alternation (Guideline 2), and enabled the studio to vet AI outputs before polish began (Guideline 1). For the live pilot project, we used Adobe Photoshop's Generative Fill feature, which was BETA-launched two weeks prior.

Fig 2. "To-Be" workflow: Reducing perpetual client involvement in the production phase was my goal.

Implementation

—

How did the blueprint unfold in real time?

1

Tone Alignment: Rather than waiting for a brief, we opened with a facilitated alignment session. It also requires trust the studio has to earn. This session was the highest-risk moment in the workflow.

2

Scene Breakdown: The boundary between AI-appropriate and human-led assets isn't always obvious. The PM and Design Lead worked out per shot what AI could reliably produce.

3

Designing with AI: Generative Fill was in beta, so evaluation criteria had to come from the Design Lead. Junior designers mass-produced environments while sketch artists handled characters.

4

QA & Compositing to Edit Floor: Final assets were reviewed against the established creative direction, then exported as layered passes to give the edit team flexibility for animation and VFX.

What I Learned

Our idea still needed designers to evaluate AI outputs to ensure quality and usability. This resulted in difficult conversations early on as the Creative Director had to actively manage client expectations around that.
Telling AI to "create a Pre Viz video with [INSERT YOUR CONCEPT] concept" can give you a Pre Viz while you order at a drive-thru. But when AI reaches its saturation point, telling AI to "make it different" is not guaranteed to work.
Oversimplified AI approaches often erase critical labour and torpedo training avenues for mentorship and workforce development. Which is why, in the live project, junior designers were tasked with using AI. Not to cut costs, but so they would develop design thinking skills rather than learning tools that AI would eventually replace.
The live pilot project did deliver 15 days ahead of when we planned to deliver, despite building the process in real time.
Update: Studies by researchers around the world tell us that hybrid workflows are better than full automation for creative tasks.
