The ECE department had an immediate gap on their website: Industry engagement.

While ECE maintained strong relationships with major companies, the website did not communicate this value clearly. Marketing needed a dedicated Industry Opportunities page that could support sponsorship discussions and augment outreach.

We had to work on this before the 2025 ECE Home Page redesign began, as we needed high value places on the home page to send site visitors to.

Disclaimer: As this public website continues to evolve, the page may differ from the versions shown. I have since left the organization, and any updates made thereafter are at their discretion.

Defining the Value

Before opening Figma, I looked at how other top-tier engineering faculties (Waterloo, UBC) structured their industry engagement.

This was an original page, so we didn't have an existing content architecture, page layout, or content to work off of. The Content was prepared by the marketing team and I had to find layouts that fit the content I had. Yes, the Content could be modified, but we knew what we wanted to show.

Research across nearly 40 North American universities, including the top 15 engineering schools (which includes us), showed that while content such as co-op, capstone projects, and industry networking is largely similar, presentation strongly influences perceived value. The examples below highlight two of ECE’s closest competitors within Canada.

Key Observations

• UBC Applied science and ECE both treat Capstone design competitions as a process (how to submit).

• Waterloo’s site relies on its reputation, but the site itself is text-heavy.

• UBC has two different sub pages that contain nearly identical descriptions of the Co-op program.

• Waterloo's business opportunities page is very Legal/Business-heavy; focused on patents and licensing.

My Process

Our goal was to create a page for corporate partners and sponsors to arrive at.

  1. Content Audit & Discovery Research

    Reviewed industry-facing pages from leading engineering institutions, including MIT and the University of Waterloo.

  2. Wireframing & Layout Design

    Created wireframes and mockups to map content flow and guide users through key information.

  3. Stakeholder Presentations & Refining

    Shared designs with leadership and iterated based on feedback from admins, students, and marketing.

  4. Complete Build to LIVE site

    Built modular sections in WordPress, added animations, and custom code where needed.

UI Design

Key UI Decisions

• The header and introductory text are used to define the scope of the page.

• Uses a two-column card layout to categorize by intent: "Work with us" for recruitment/networking, "Sponsor us" for financial support.

• Uses a three-column grid to organize information on talent pipelines, brand visibility, and research expertise.

• Presents departmental metrics that define the organization's standing.

• Provides direct contact information required to initiate a partnership.

Web Development

I built this page out on our WordPress while following our University's brand guidelines. Components were intentionally modular so marketing/web production designer (then, it was also Me) could update statistics, images, or content without risking layout integrity or developer dependency.

Why This Page Mattered

Although designed for industry, the page intentionally served two audiences.
The page functioned as a clear value proposition for Industry partners - that was the goal. It told organizations why they should sponsor research or recruit from ECE, using credibility signals such as number of patents and student invention disclosures, rather than dense academic language or overt marketing.
While secondary, the student impact was deliberate. Visibility into industry partnerships helped justify ECE’s career outcomes and positioned the department as invested in student employability.
This page was one of three launches in December 2024, alongside Student Research Opportunities and Latest News. By building these pages first, I made sure that when I eventually redesigned the homepage a month later, we had places to send people.
