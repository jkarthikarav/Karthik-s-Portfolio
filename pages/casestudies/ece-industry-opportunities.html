The Context

Marketing needed a dedicated Industry Opportunities page that could support sponsorship discussions and augment outreach.

This was an original page, the ECE had never had anything like on their website before.

This is the page I built: Industry Opportunities .

We had to work on this before the 2025 ECE Home Page redesign began, as we needed high value places on the home page to send site visitors to.

We didn't have an existing content architecture, page layout, or content to work off of.

The Content was prepared by the marketing team and while the Content could be modified, we knew exactly what we wanted to show.

Content Audit & Discovery Research

I looked at how other top-tier engineering faculties (Waterloo, UBC) structured their industry engagement.

Research across nearly 40 North American universities, including the top 15 engineering schools (which includes us), showed that while content such as co-op, capstone projects, and industry networking is largely similar, presentation strongly influences perceived value.

Key Observations from 2 Competitors

• UBC Applied sciences treats Capstone design competitions as a process, i.e., how to submit.

• Waterloo’s site is text-heavy.

• UBC has two different sub pages for the Co-op program.

• Waterloo's business opportunities page focuses on patents and licensing.

The Solution

I built this page out on WordPress, following our University's brand guidelines. The mockup can be seen below:

Intent-based categorization

By utilizing a two-column card layout to distinguish "Work with us" from "Sponsor us," the interface allows professional stakeholders to self-identify their goals immediately. This structural choice reduces cognitive load and ensures that it remains focused on the partnership rather than an information dump.

Information grids

This organizes value, like talent pipelines, brand visibility, and research expertise, into digestible chunks of information. Moving from a broad intentions to a detailed service list ensures the page remains functionally dense but also suitable for partners.

Quantifiable proof

By integrating specific departmental metrics, the page provides the social proof required. To move beyond the feel of a standard web form, the content prioritizes the final call to action as a professional inquiry rather than a passive submission.

Why This Page Mattered

It told organizations why they should sponsor research or recruit from ECE, using credibility signals such as number of patents and student invention disclosures, rather than dense academic language or overt marketing.
While secondary, the student impact was deliberate. Visibility into industry partnerships helped justify ECE’s career outcomes and positioned the department as invested in student employability.
This page was one of three launches in December 2024, alongside Student Research Opportunities and Latest News. By building these pages first, I made sure that when I eventually redesigned the homepage a month later, we had places to send people.
