The Challenge

The bosses at Fix It In Post Studios wondered: "What was the future of Film Production under AI-enabled technological disruption?"

In June 2023, A movie director approached Fix It In Post Studios to create an animated Pre-viz video to help market their Film to Potential Investors.

A Pre-Viz is used to align producers and other crew members before committing to full production. This made it an ironically suitable project to test AI-assisted production.

A standard Pre-Viz was a well-established project type with existing workflows. Earlier (in 2020-21) when I used to be a full time employee, I'd worked on similar projects with other directors.

FIIP Studios had asked me to design a service to make GenAI-assisted production billable, and I suggested using this project as a live implementation case for the design guidelines made previously.

I approached it as a service design problem, applying the four design guidelines established in Phase 1 to structure every decision, instead of simply telling the team to use Photoshop's new Generative Fill feature.

The Karthik Approach

Firstly, I studied the Current "As-Is" Workflow.

The traditional Pre-viz creation process is what we would call a "bottleneck."

It kept the studio in a reactive, freelance-dependent environment, where the studio couldn't move until the Director did the heavy lifting.

Fig 1. "As-Is" workflow where the Director retained Creative control, and the headaches.

— Key Pain Points

The Current Workflow had these issues:

If a Director couldn't articulate their vision, the studio remained idle.

Without a clear division between environment and character assets, any creative change required a massive restart.

Vague briefs and long silences led to excessive last-minute changes.

The Karthik Approach

Secondly, Due to my research, we knew that fully automated workflows were risky due to the lack of control.

I proposed a copilot-style workflow.

Even though Photoshop's Generative Fill feature was BETA-launched two weeks prior, we were prepared for the randomness by tasking Designers with prompt structures & visual identity beforehand.

This approach made AI do all the heavy lifting (generating textures, backgrounds, props) while the studio's employees would work on composition and visual polishes.

Fig 2. "To-Be" workflow where the studio had more control over the output.

Workflow Implementation

I managed the pilot program to test the new workflow using a live project, ensuring the copilot-style logic worked in a high-pressure environment.

The Stress-Test validated that AI, when embedded inside a structured workflow, could reduce bottlenecks in Pre-viz without compromising creative ownership. Each phase was scoped by applying Guideline 3; AI was assigned only to specific stages (background generation, textures, props) rather than the entire pipeline.

Tone Alignment

The Editor and Client align on tone at the beginning.

Visual reference used to establish creative direction and expectations.

Scene Breakdown

The Project Manager and Design Lead break the scene into assets that require AI generation, such as backgrounds and textures, versus assets requiring human precision, such as logos, text, and specific characters.

Layer logic defined before asset production begins.

Designing with AI

While sketch artists worked on character development, designers used generative AI to mass-produce environmental assets, refining selected outputs through image manipulation in Photoshop.

AI-generated backgrounds refined for production use.

QA, Approval, Compositing, Pass to Edit

Final images are sent for approval and, once validated, exported as layered passes to the editor. Delivering separate passes enables downstream animation and motion control within the edit.

Layered exports prepared for motion and compositing.

The Karthik Approach

Finally, with the real-time client project, I achieved the client's goal of making GenAI-assisted production billable, while also future-proofing.

The resulting blueprint shifted the studio from a reactive, client action-dependent model to a proactive, studio-led service structure.

By building the backstage, including structured GenAI prompts, QA checkpoints, and task/sub-task boundaries, the studio gained control over output quality and delivery. Importantly, the system was designed to integrate AI into existing workflows without undermining morale or creative ownership.

The following blueprint has been rigorously validated over the pilot program, and barring project-specific requirements, has been used As-Is.

Lessons from Early GenAI Adoption

In 2023, as Generative AI entered the commercial market, this project shows that successful adoption depends less on the AI itself and more on workflow design. The final deliverable was indistinguishable from traditionally produced work, giving the client early-adopter credibility without compromising perceived quality.
This live implementation accelerated organizational learning and surfaced technical pain points that a theoretical blueprint would have missed. By acting as both Service Designer and Pilot Project Manager, I was able to iterate the workflow in real time and translate principles into team-level systems. Most importantly, Core creative direction remained human-led.
As a result, the studio strengthened their own credibility through early, structured AI adoption, achieving approximately 30–40% revenue growth from repeat projects and referrals, reducing project timelines by around two weeks, and significantly lowering client anxiety and last-minute tensions while improving employee morale.
