UX Research Internship at Caterpillar UX Research · Wireframes Timeline: Dec 2019

Generating Actionable Insights for Product Design: A Construction Management tool

Caterpillar needed research on construction site supervisors. I had no construction industry knowledge, asked to be covert, and two weeks to produce findings worth presenting to a UX lead. My contract was extended by a month. This is what that internship looked like.

01    Project Context
I was asked to conduct research with construction site supervisors without disclosing that I was a Caterpillar intern. I ended up discovering a pain point within their workflows — communicating progress to their clients — and designed a mobile app/SaaS dashboard to help solve it.

I was an intern in my third year of my university program in Media Studies, but at Caterpillar, I was hired as a UX Intern. What I got out of my research was what I got out of it. There was no upper ceiling, but the bottom of the pit was to far deep.

I had no background in construction and no familiarity with how sites operated, nor did I have prior knowledge of the workflows, hierarchies, or pressures that defined a site supervisor's day.

02    Approach
When participants can't know who you work for, you can't rely on their name to open conversations. Instead, you have to be genuine and trustable. I approached site supervisors as someone trying to understand their work (which I was) and let my preparation show that I was worth talking to.

Before any field research could happen, I needed enough domain knowledge to ask credible questions and recognize meaningful answers. I studied construction project structures such as initiation, planning, execution, closing; resource categories including workforce, equipment, raw materials; and scheduling, and the key metrics site supervisors were accountable for: duration, budget, safety, quality.

I did the following UX processes:

Observational Interviews, on-site

Empathy mapping

User flow documentation

03    The Findings
Site supervisors don't need more data. They need the right data, at the right moment, without leaving the site to get it.

Both supervisor profiles pointed toward the same underlying need: a way to plan, track, and communicate progress.

The key findings of my research were that any solution should:

Prioritize Scheduling & Task Tracking

Reduce Manual Resource Tracking

Reduce Reactive Client Communication

Ensure Connectivity throughout Systems

05    What Happened After
After presenting to the UX lead, my contract was extended by a month to shadow agile sessions. I was now watching how research findings move through a product team in practice.

The most important thing I learned on this project wasn't about construction. It was about domain fluency as a research skill. You don't need to be an expert in a field to do good research in it. But you do need to know enough to ask questions that earn respect and recognize answers that matter.

The weeks following the research completion, I designed a mobile app and a SaaS Dashboard in Figma. Recently, with the help of AI, I privately built them out in front-end code to demonstrate how they'd actually look and behave, not just how they'd appear in a static mockup.
