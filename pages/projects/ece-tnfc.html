Rebuilding a Standalone Website into a Department-Hosted Content Hub.

I researched, designed, prototyped, and built end to end – reimagining ECE at U of T's TNFC website for Nanofabrication-focused researchers, delivered as a responsive content hub working across phone, tablet, and desktop integrated into ECE's main public-facing website.

Despite being located inside the ECE department, the Toronto Nanofabrication centre (TNFC) – one of U of T's numerous research labs – had its own CMS infrastructure, and no connection to ECE's brand or navigation. My work involved consolidating this multi-page site into a two-page content hub-style section inside of ECE's digital environment.

In Nov 2025, I was tasked with conducting research and auditing the standalone site to understand how to improve the user experience on the site and to propose a design-focused solution to integrate the TNFC lab into ECE's environment. The goal was to make a digital experience that feels as modern and easy to use as any institutional website, built on two core principles: Clarity — help people understand what TNFC is about. Simplicity — remove unnecessary friction from key tasks such as downloading and accessing forms.

The Figma prototype and initial CMS/Web development phase of the TNFC Homepage and its core sub-page took less than 2 weeks.

01    I Proposed a Solution that addressed Content Needs.

The original website's pages did not exist because the information required that space. Therefore, stripping away the auto-generated architecture revealed two genuine content needs: a home page that explained what TNFC was and what it offered, and an access page that gave users everything they needed to book and use the facility.

Therefore, my Proposal sought to address these points:

Resolve standalone site identity

Increase content discoverability

Reduce maintenance overhead

Align with ECE branding

Simplify navigation structure

02    A new home experience.

TNFC's original home page reads like an institutional bulletin board. The biggest disconnect was the visual consistency. U of T’s brand materials emphasize a coherent visual identity and brand voice across websites, while the original page uses a very plain layout, weak hierarchy, and a dated left-content/right-sidebar blog structure that doesn’t feel like a modern institutional service site. The page hierarchy is also weak because the News & Events column competes with the core explanation of what TNFC is and why a user should care.

My design focused on making it be perceived as open, active, and easy to engage with, instead of users having to hunt through disconnected sections.

04    Reducing Click-through Depth by removing redundancy.

Most of the top-level pages were auto-generated link aggregators, not real content pages. The actual information was buried in sub-pages that users had to navigate to independently.

Take User Access, an important page on the site. The five subpages had content that could be consolidated into a single page without having to erase or deprioritize any critical information.

05    Improving Recognition with Visual Displays.

TNFC had information about partner organizations, which instead of being easy to recall, was originally a text-based list of hyperlinks on a Partners subpage under About Us.

I integrated it as a three-column section with an auto-scrolling logo gallery on the TNFC home page, improving partner recognition.

06    Designed for every screen, built for every student.

No one interacts with a website from a single device. TNFC was designed responsively from the first frame, delivering a consistent experience across mobile, tablet, laptop, and desktop.

Every layout, component, and content section was built to adapt fluidly across screen sizes, ensuring navigation remained intuitive and information remained readable regardless of how students accessed the site.

Reflections

Every design decision was driven by the need to ensure ease of access to the regulations, including access, training, billing, and safety. At the same time, the pages were designed to feel native to ECE while also expressing TNFC’s identity and values. The result positions TNFC as a core, high-value research facility within the department.

After I left, development on other parts of the site reused components and patterns that were originally designed for TNFC's section, including the Grid Modernization Centre's new page. The patterns were picked up and used after I left — not only were my components and patterns liked — the structural decisions made sense and they were also repeatable.
