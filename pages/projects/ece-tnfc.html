Part of ECE Web Production

Leading the Website Modernization and Systems migration of Toronto Nanofabrication Centre's (TNFC) website

TNFC had its own website with separate infrastructure, inconsistent branding, and no connection to ECE's digital environment. I consolidated a five-top-level-page site with sub-pages each into a two-page section that felt native to ECE without erasing TNFC's identity.

Role: UX Research · Content Design · Front-End Development Timeline: Nov 2024 – Mar 2025 Tools: Figma · WordPress
The Problem

A research lab that looked like a separate organization.

TNFC is one of U of T's research labs/facilities that is located inside the ECE department. But its website existed outside ECE's digital ecosystem entirely with different infrastructure, different visual language, no connection to ECE's brand or navigation.

It just hadn't been brought in yet. Most of the top-level pages were auto-generated link aggregators, not real content pages. The actual information was buried in sub-pages that users had to navigate to independently. There was also a separate news and blog channel.

The requirement was clear: bring TNFC into ECE's environment. How to do that without destroying the lab's identity or overwhelming ECE's information architecture was the design problem.

The TNFC homepage when the project was greenlit.

The Audit

Before deciding what to build, I had to decide what to keep.

I audited the full existing site, every top-level page, every sub-page, every content item, against two questions: does this need its own page, or can it exist somewhere else? And if it needs its own page, what does that page actually need to do?

Most of the site's complexity was structural. The pages did not exist because the information required that much space. Stripping away the auto-generated architecture revealed two genuine content needs: a home page that explained what TNFC was and what it offered, and an access page that gave users everything they needed to book and use the facility.

Meanwhile, the news and blog channel was the easiest call: merge it into ECE Latest (a page I built for news and blogs) with a TNFC category tag.

The Recommendation

I recommended two pages.

The Facilities content, for example, was a subpage. I proposed integrating it as a structured grid section on the TNFC home page, reducing click depth for one of the lab's most-visited content types.

TNFC's Facilities Page

What it looks like now on the TNFC Home

The Access page consolidated all forms, processes, policies, and related documents in one place. Every compliance and regulatory requirement (booking, training, billing, safety) had a home that was findable without knowing where to look.

My original proposal included equipment capabilities as a section on the home page, rather than a full page. After I left, the organization extended the architecture to add a third standalone page for that content. My structure was extensible enough to absorb it.

UX Writing

On most projects I've worked on, content comes with a direction.

On TNFC, the content had to be written from the audit findings up. I had to determine how TNFC should describe itself within ECE's ecosystem, what hierarchy the information should follow, and also write for two different audiences simultaneously.

Audience A

Researchers who already knew TNFC

Audience B

Prospective users, encountering it for the first time

I wrote the initial content and handed it to marketing to refine.

That handoff itself was a design decision: making sure what I handed over was specific enough to improve.

The Outcome

An architecture extensible enough to outlast the project.

The pages shipped as part of the ECE rebuild in March 2025. After I left, development on other parts of the ECE site reused components and patterns that were originally designed for TNFC's section, including the Grid Modernization Centre's new page.

The architecture was picked up and extended without me. That's the proof that the structural decisions were right.

What I Learned

Choosing to consolidate five pages into two was an IA decision. Deciding what each of those two pages needed to say, and in what order, couldn't be made independently.

This was not just a website design. This was about designing content and unifying digital systems to reduce administrative work and signal a cohesive brand identity.

Every design decision was driven by compliance and regulation, including access, training, billing, and safety. At the same time, the pages were designed to feel native to ECE while clearly articulating TNFC’s value. The result positions TNFC as a core, high-value research facility within the department.
