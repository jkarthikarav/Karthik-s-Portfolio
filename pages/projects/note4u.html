Cross-Disciplinary Project

Research-Led Product Strategy for an AI Music Transcription Tool

In a cross-disciplinary course meant for business, law, and music graduate students, I used UX research to shape a business proposal and support a product strategy for an AI music transcription tool.

Deliverables: UX Research · Product Strategy Timeline: Sep–Dec 2024 Course: JDM3619 Digital Media Distribution
The Problem

Every team made screens. I made the case for who would buy them.

This was a cross-disciplinary course at the University of Toronto that typically brings together students from business, law, music, and UX to build a digital music business proposal together. I was the UX student on our team.

Most teams used their UX student to add interface thinking like wireframes, screens, product concepts. I chose to contribute differently: instead of producing more screens, I focused the research on the business questions the team needed answered. Who is this actually for and how much AI will they accept before it stops feeling like their music?

Concept visualization created with Figma Make after the project concluded

My Contribution

I used UX research to answer business questions, not design ones.

The product concept was Note4U, an AI-assisted tool that converts musical ideas into sheet music or MIDI files. The business problem was: who is this for, and how do you reach them in a market full of DAW integrations, standalone tools, and subscription-based competitors?

I ran secondary research and use insights from interviews with students, amateur producers, and professional musicians.

I synthesized research findings into a target customer profile and journey map that helped the team evaluate positioning, pricing, and go-to-market opportunities. I mapped the current journey to identify where Note4U could create value, which informed positioning, partnership opportunities, adoption strategy, and future product decisions.

Assessing Product Risks

Just because a Market for a feature exists, doesn't mean that your Product can compete.

We were strongest on value and business viability, as those were the primary criteria we were judged upon.

We did focus on the whole roadmap and self-assessed where our proposal was complete versus where it still had open questions.

Value

Will they buy it?

Usability

Can they use it?

We addressed the Value as our competitive analysis showed existing tools either require DAW expertise or are too expensive. The persona work and user research identified a specific frustrated user and the journey map shows the cost of the current workaround including time, money, and dependency on others. The one-time purchase model directly responds to the finding that subscription pricing was a barrier.

The VST integration angle: fitting into tools people already use, is a Usability decision, but it wasn't validated with users.

Feasibility

Can we build it?

Viability

Should we build it?

Our proposal addressed Feasibility by proposing cloud-based infrastructure and VST distribution via Native Instruments or Kontakt. But the core AI transcription capabilities: how accurate it actually is, what the error rate looks like, whether it works across different instruments and genres, isn't validated.

The business model canvas, revenue streams, pricing tiers, partnership strategy, investor targets, and competitive positioning we presented all speak to the Viability of the business directly.

The Key Insight

Just because AI can create music doesn't mean it should.

The frustration users felt wasn't only about slow transcription, it was about their creative process. Tool dependent and out of their control. So how much AI could the product include without breaking them?

The research made clear that AI needed to assist the creative process, not replace the creation. Note4U's value wasn't that it could generate sheet music independently.

We didn't want users feeling like the music wasn't theirs anymore.

Design Work Beyond the Scope of This Project

This project focused on understanding the opportunity, validating the business case, and identifying where Note4U could create value for musicians. While we discussed product features and adoption strategies, detailed product design and usability validation were outside the scope of the course.

If the project were taken further, the next step would be translating the research findings into product decisions. That would involve exploring information architecture, interaction patterns, and workflows around tasks like audio transcription, MIDI export, and sheet music generation, followed by usability testing to validate those decisions with users.
