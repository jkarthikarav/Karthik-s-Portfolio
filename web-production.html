Website Production Design

    June 2024 - March 2025 • Work Highlights • Web Design, UX, WordPress, Figma

Summary: I maintained and built various key pages for the University of Toronto’s ECE Department website, improving accessibility, usability, and aligning key page content with administrative goals -- while designing on Figma and implementing on WordPress.

I worked at the University of Toronto’s ECE Department, redesigning and managing their public-facing website and intranet, combining accessibility auditing, content and visual design in Figma, stakeholder coordination, and end-to-end WordPress implementation. Overall, my core responsibilities were to:

Maintain the ECE's public website and their intranet while coordinating with internal stakeholders and teams for content updates.

This redesign matched the department’s communication goals, making ECE’s research, events, and learning opportunities easier to understand for different audiences. Each update helped improve the department’s online identity, allowing faculty and staff to share information more clearly and giving students and industry visitors a better view of the department’s work. The site’s structure and tone were adjusted to show the professionalism and academic standards of the University of Toronto. This process allowed the design to stay responsive to short‑term departmental updates while maintaining long‑term usability standards.

My Approach

01  Understand

Studied the website to understand how students, faculty, and visitors navigate and make decisions online. Applying Hick’s Law, I simplified information paths so users could locate what they need faster and feel less overwhelmed.

02  Examine

Audited the content and layouts to see how design patterns influenced readability and comprehension. Using Gestalt Principles, such as proximity and similarity, I made sure items were identifiable visually to reduce cognitive friction.

03  Reframe

Designed and refined mockups based on feedback, focusing on how users interacted. Fitts’s Law and Jakob’s Law were important, Component/section layout design and User movement/interactivity repsectively.

What I did

Building and QA for web

As part of my role, I frequently created mockups in Figma to visualize and refine design changes before implementating them in WordPress. These ranged from full-page redesigns to smaller, incremental updates where I adjusted layouts, components, or added new content blocks to existing pages.

Each version reflected changes in the department’s content goals, shaped by feedback from faculty, marketing, and admin. Design consistency was often balanced with urgent content needs or limits within WordPress. Stakeholder input sometimes shifted the focus from style to clarity, or from redesigning pages to improving navigation. These ongoing changes made the workflow flexible and adaptive instead of linear.

This process allowed stakeholders to preview changes early and provide feedback before any development work was done. By iterating through mockups first, I helped the department save time on revisions and ship pages that were both user-friendly and visually consistent with U of T's branding guidelines (available publicly here).

Some Pages like the Events page were just the default WordPress widgets themselves. However, unless you are willing to invest time in developing a custom events widget, the limitations of WordPress' default widgets quickly become apparent. The new Events layout made it easier for visitors to find and follow upcoming opportunities and deadlines instead of just viewing static listings. This change turned a simple content area into an active space that encouraged participation and improved student awareness of events. The updates focused on clarity, using clear structure and consistent layouts so users could find and act on information easily.

Identifying opportunities with website content

I also updated content directly on the website, uploaded new blog posts, and modified images to ensure consistency with the department’s branding and design standards. These updates helped keep the site current and engaging for students and visitors.

I was also responsible for building a new page to highlight news articles, blog posts, and other related content here. While I did not do the research and design for this page, I did work on the other end of it: implementing the UX/UI designs and regularly uploading Blogs (with content from the Marketing Team).
